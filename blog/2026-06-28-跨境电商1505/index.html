Best Things to Buy Right Now — June 28, 2026

Quick take: Today is not a sale day. It is a liability day. Three separate platform changes take effect this Sunday, and every one of them moves the answer to the same question: who is responsible when your order goes missing, arrives broken, or turns out to be nothing like the listing? eBay’s rewritten user agreement goes live today and hands the entire shipping leg — and the compensation for it — to the platform. A short-video marketplace reclassified live-auction wins as final sale, so a whole category of impulse buys just lost its change-of-mind return. And a low-price giant kicked off a three-week discount ladder that starts today and steps up on July 4. Meanwhile the round of 32 opens this afternoon, the biggest driving week of the American year is six days out, and on Wednesday a stack of cross-border costs switch on across Europe. Below: what actually changed, who now carries the risk, and twenty things worth buying while the old cost base still holds.

What’s Hot This Week

The single most useful thing that happened this week is invisible on a product page. eBay’s new user agreement takes effect today, and it is a bigger deal for buyers than for the sellers complaining about it. Every seller must now ship on platform-issued labels. Your shipping payment goes to the marketplace rather than to the seller, and on free-shipping listings the cost is deducted from the seller’s balance. In exchange, the platform now explicitly compensates for loss and damage in transit and steps in directly when an item never arrives. Translation: on that marketplace, “the seller says they posted it” stopped being your problem this morning. You also get to pick standard, expedited, or carrier-point pickup at checkout on eligible items, which was previously whatever the seller felt like offering.

The flip side landed on short-video commerce, and it cuts the other way. Live-auction wins have been reclassified as final-sale items, with a label now shown on the buyer page. If you win a bid and then decide you misclicked, no longer want it, or found it cheaper elsewhere, you cannot return it for a change of mind. Faulty goods and not-as-described claims still stand. This is a rational fix to a real abuse problem, but it changes how you should shop those rooms: auctions are now a commitment, not a browsing activity. If you have been treating live bidding as entertainment with an escape hatch, the hatch closed. Bid on things you would buy at that price on a normal product page.

A three-tier discount ladder started today and runs to mid-July. The low-price marketplace launched a new recurring promotional format this morning with three depth tiers: 15% and 20% off running from today through July 19, and a deeper 30% tier that does not open until July 4 and closes on July 12. That structure is worth reading carefully, because it tells you exactly how to sequence your buying. Anything you need before Independence Day, buy in the 20% tier now. Anything that can wait a week, wait for the 30% window — it is only eight days long and it is deliberately timed against the holiday. Consumables, storage, car accessories and cheap organisation goods are where the deeper tier will actually bite.

The knockout stage opens today, and it has pulled a very specific basket of goods with it. The round of 32 begins this afternoon and runs through Friday, which means seven straight days of afternoon and evening matches across three North American time zones. Combined with a Saturday July 4 holiday, this is the densest week of at-home hosting and outdoor watching in the calendar. Seating, portable audio, coolers, shade and anything battery-powered is running hot, and — crucially — the demand is being served out of domestic warehouses, which is why prices on those categories are still sane despite the surge.

Beauty is having its most competitive month in two years, and the competition is good for you. On the US short-video leaderboard, a Korean lip balm and a mass-market lip product from a rival label tied for the top spot in mid-June at roughly $3.1 to $3.8 million each — the first time in months the number one position has been shared. Below them, the collagen set that led the first quarter has now declined for three straight months. A UK three-piece skincare gift set with eighteen scent options posted a genuinely absurd week: 14,600 sets sold in seven days, up more than 22,000% week on week, around £363,500 at a £31 price point. And the fastest-growing single ingredient on the platform is kojic acid, with 30-day video views up 1,600% and roughly 90,500 monthly searches. When four different formulas are all fighting for the same shelf, the discounting gets real. Buy beauty this month, not next.

The cheapest genuine bargain of the week is a nine-dollar eyeliner. A cross-border storefront’s stamp-style eyeliner pen — one end draws, the other stamps a wing in a single press — has moved over 180,000 units since it launched last September and has repeatedly topped the beauty chart. It sells for about $9. It is the clearest example of the current playbook on that platform: take a skill people find hard, remove the skill requirement entirely, and demonstrate it in eight seconds of video. Products built on that logic are almost always underpriced early, because they have no brand to defend.

And the quiet story: a batch of Chinese brands nobody in the West has heard of just posted absurd mid-year numbers on the open marketplace. The mid-year event report published this week showed brand GMV up 90% year on year, 177 brands growing more than threefold versus the March event, and brand penetration approaching 40% of transactions. The names in the winners list are not household ones: a 3D-printer brand up sixteenfold year on year in Brazil, a pool-cleaning robot brand clearing $200,000 in Spain alone, a game-controller brand up more than tenfold in Brazil, a carbon cycling-wheel brand, and a car-diagnostics brand up fourfold in Chile and 2.8-fold in Poland. For buyers, that is the most useful list of the month. These are categories where the Western branded option costs two to four times as much for functionally similar hardware, and where the brands are now big enough to have real warranty and support operations rather than being anonymous drop-shippers.

Wednesday is a hard wall for anything crossing into Europe. On July 1 the EU’s €150 duty-free threshold for low-value imports disappears and is replaced by a flat €3 customs charge — and the detail that matters is that it is levied per tariff line, not per parcel. A box containing only T-shirts is charged €3. A box containing a T-shirt, a candle and a serum is charged €9. A separate €2 handling fee per line is expected by 1 November, taking the combined charge to €5 per item type. The interim regime is scheduled to run until mid-2028, when full classification-based duties arrive. If you are buying into an EU address, the arithmetic now punishes mixed baskets. One category per order, bought today, is materially cheaper than a variety box bought Thursday.

Platform & Category Watch

eBay. Beyond today’s shipping overhaul, the bigger buyer event is three days away. On July 1 the fee structure is replaced: the old tiered final-value system becomes flat category rates — around 7% on whole electronics and computing, 12% on accessories, 14% on home, sport and fashion — and, critically, certain used and refurbished conditions drop to about 5%, a cut of up to 60%. Sellers with below-average service ratings and elevated return rates will pay a surcharge on top, rising further if they do not improve within four months. Two things follow from that. First, if you want refurbished electronics, wait until Wednesday — a 5% take rate on a $400 laptop is roughly $28 of margin handed back, and in a competitive category that shows up in the price within days. Second, the service-rating surcharge means the platform is now financially penalising sellers with bad returns records, so the star ratings on seller pages have real teeth for the first time. Separately, the UK motors and parts category has had 30-day change-of-mind free returns since June 8, with the platform rewriting old listings automatically and subsidising half the return label; buyer’s-remorse returns on car parts there are currently the easiest they have ever been.

Amazon. Two deadlines bracket this week. From June 30, every listing sold into the EU and UK must carry a declared country of origin, whether fulfilled by the platform or by the seller — incomplete listings face restricted cross-border delivery or removal. Expect some listings to simply vanish from cross-border results on Tuesday. If there is something specific you have been watching from an overseas seller into a European address, buy it today or accept that it may be unavailable for a week or two while the data gets fixed. Then on July 8, a US filing requirement kicks in for regulated imports — children’s products, a swathe of home goods, and anything with a battery — requiring certificate data to be submitted through customs systems on arrival. That one will thin out the long tail of unbranded battery-powered gadgets over the summer, which is a safety improvement and a price increase at the same time.

Also worth knowing: the variation review-sharing policy is now fully in force. Colour and pattern variants, functionally identical size variants, pack-count variants and model-fit variants still pool their reviews. Variants that differ functionally no longer do. The practical effect is that some listings you looked at in May with 4,000 reviews now show 400. The product did not get worse; the review count got honest. Do not let the drop scare you off, and equally, stop treating a huge review count on a multi-variant listing as proof that the specific variant you are buying is any good. Finally, the company has publicly backed extending the UK’s deemed-reseller VAT collection rules to all third-party sellers rather than only overseas ones — an estimated £3.2 billion of annual platform sales is currently slipping through the gap.

Short-video commerce. The US mid-year event runs June 17 to July 3, so five days remain. The June 26 auction day, boosted by a 3% commission rebate, pushed cross-border auction livestreams past ten thousand sessions in a single day, roughly triple last year. That volume is where clearance actually happens: slow-moving standardised goods with lots of attributes — car fasteners, phone cases, mixed jewellery lots — cleared hardest. Just remember today’s rule change before you bid. In the UK, the weekly leaderboard is a different world from the US one: top-ten items ran between roughly £1.96 million and £6.63 million, dominated by skincare sets, folding treadmills, high-protein snacks and baby care — small-flat, health-conscious, practical. Mexico’s chart is led by audio-visual gear and sportswear. The US chart is beauty first, then car accessories, summer dresses and cleaning appliances. If you shop across borders, those three charts are effectively three different stores.

The low-price marketplace. Alongside today’s discount ladder, the wider strategy shift continues: US advertising spend has been cut hard this year, with budget consolidating into fewer channels while monthly active users still grew over 20% year on year. That is a retention play, and retention plays are good for existing customers. Fewer spectacular first-order coupons, better standing prices on repeat purchases. Storage, organisation, cables, car interior accessories and disposables are where the value concentrates.

The open marketplace. Beyond the mid-year brand numbers, the useful development is that the “hidden champion” brands now hold enough volume to run proper storefronts with warranty terms, spare parts and firmware support. That was not true two years ago. For hardware categories where you would previously have paid a Western brand premium purely for support — diagnostics tools, controllers, entry-level 3D printers, pool robots — the calculus has genuinely changed this year.

Walmart. The removal of the lifetime sales threshold for seller access to platform fulfilment has widened the pool of marketplace sellers shipping from domestic warehouses with two-day delivery. For this specific week, that matters more than usual: anything you order for the July 4 weekend needs to be domestically stocked to arrive, and the marketplace-versus-first-party delivery gap has narrowed to near zero on home, outdoor and small appliances.

Shopify and the independent web. Two things happened at the end of this month that will reshape small-brand storefronts. The Spring 2026 release shipped with more than 150 changes built around AI agents and an open commerce protocol, meaning independent-store catalogues are increasingly published in structured form and readable by assistants — you can already ask an assistant to find and check out a product on a small brand’s own site without visiting it. And on June 30, the legacy checkout scripting system stops executing entirely. Expect a rough few days on smaller independent stores next week: tiered discounts, volume pricing, buy-one-get-one rules, free-gift thresholds and custom shipping logic built on the old system will silently stop firing on stores that did not migrate. If a discount code you know exists fails on an independent site on July 1 or 2, it is probably not you. Email the brand — most will honour it manually.

Compliance, and why it is a shopping issue. The reason the EU moved its parcel rules forward by two years is worth knowing as a buyer. Nearly 5.9 billion low-value items entered the bloc duty-free from outside it last year. In 2025 testing across the 27 member states covering toys, electronics, cosmetics, protective equipment and dietary supplements, more than 60% of sampled goods failed to meet entry standards — missing compliance labelling, banned ingredients, or no safety certification at all. That is the real argument for buying the categories on that list from a brand with an address and a warranty rather than from the cheapest listing on a search page. It costs more. It is also the only version of this where you know what is in the bottle.

Buying Tips for This Week

Sequence your discount tiers instead of buying everything today. The three-tier ladder that opened this morning is designed to hold you across two purchase moments. The 15% and 20% tiers run to July 19; the 30% tier only exists between July 4 and July 12. Anything with a holiday deadline goes in the first basket. Anything that is genuinely just restocking — cables, storage, disposables, cleaning supplies — goes on a list for July 4. The gap is worth real money on a basket of twenty cheap items.

On refurbished electronics, being three days patient is the highest-return decision on this page. A fee cut of up to 60% on qualifying used and refurbished conditions lands Wednesday. Sellers in that category run on thin margins and compete almost entirely on price, so the pass-through is fast. Shortlist today, buy Wednesday.

Buy your EU cross-border order today, and buy it single-category. From Wednesday the charge is per tariff line. If you have been holding a basket with a shirt, a book and a bottle of something in it, splitting that into three separate future orders costs €9 in new duty; buying it today costs zero. And if you must buy after Wednesday, one category per parcel is the cheapest possible shape.

Treat live auctions as final purchases from today onward. The change-of-mind return on auction wins is gone. Faulty and not-as-described claims survive, so you are still protected against actual problems — but “I got carried away” is no longer a covered condition. A simple rule: do not bid above what you would happily pay on a fixed-price listing for the same thing.

Read review counts differently this month. With variation review pooling now restricted to genuinely equivalent variants, a listing that showed thousands of reviews in the spring may show a fraction of that now. That is a correction, not a decline. Judge the number against comparable listings today, not against your memory of it.

Check the seller’s service rating on the marketplace with the new fee structure. From Wednesday, sellers rated below average with elevated return rates pay a surcharge that escalates if they do not fix it. That makes the rating a genuine financial signal rather than a vanity badge. On identical items, the higher-rated seller is now also the more solvent one.

For anything with a battery, a certification mark, or a child using it, buy the brand. More than 60% of sampled low-value imports failed EU entry standards last year across exactly those categories. The July 8 US filing requirement will thin out the worst of it, but not this week. This is the one place where the cheapest listing is genuinely the wrong answer.

If a discount code fails on a small independent store next week, it is probably their checkout, not you. The legacy scripting system that powers a lot of custom discount logic stops executing on June 30. Email the brand rather than abandoning the basket — most will apply it manually, and several will add something for the trouble.

What’s Coming Next

Monday, June 29. A self-fulfilment handling-time accuracy rule takes effect on the largest US marketplace: sellers who consistently ship faster than their stated handling time get it adjusted automatically. Delivery estimates on third-party listings get more honest in both directions from tomorrow.

Tuesday, June 30. Country-of-origin data becomes mandatory on European listings. Expect gaps in cross-border search results on Tuesday and Wednesday. Also the last day the legacy independent-store checkout scripting system runs.

Wednesday, July 1. The big one. EU low-value duty exemption ends and the €3-per-tariff-line charge begins. The marketplace fee restructure lands, with refurbished conditions dropping to roughly 5%. Corporate withholding changes take effect for sellers in parts of Southeast Asia, which will nudge prices there upward within a fortnight.

Friday, July 3. The short-video mid-year event closes. Last call on the creator-subsidised beauty pricing described above.

Saturday, July 4. Independence Day, the 30% discount tier opens on the low-price marketplace, and the round of 16 begins. Everything competes for attention on the same day, which historically produces the sloppiest and therefore best pricing of the summer.

Wednesday, July 8. US certificate filing requirements begin for regulated imports — children’s products, many home goods, and battery-powered items. Long-tail unbranded listings in those categories start disappearing after this date.

Sunday, July 19. The 15% and 20% discount tiers close, and the tournament final is played. The summer’s demand peak ends here; the post-holiday markdown season begins the following week.

Around November 1. The additional €2 per-line handling fee is expected in the EU, taking the combined per-item charge to €5. Anything you plan to import into Europe in reasonable volume is cheaper before then than after.

Price Trend Outlook

Falling over the next two weeks. Refurbished electronics, on the fee cut. Beauty and skincare, on the four-way fight at the top of the category and the last week of event subsidies. Cheap organisation, storage and car interior goods, on the discount ladder. Entry-level hardware from the open-marketplace brands, which are buying market share and have the volume to fund it.

Falling after July 19. Everything seasonal that does not sell by the final: portable seating, shade, coolers, outdoor audio, patriotic apparel. The markdowns in the last ten days of July are usually the deepest of the year on summer goods, because the storage cost of holding them until next May is brutal.

Rising from Wednesday. Anything shipped into the EU from outside it, immediately and permanently — €3 now, likely €5 from November. Multi-category parcels rise fastest in percentage terms, which is a genuinely new distortion. Battery-powered and child-facing imports into the US will drift up from July 8 as compliance costs get priced in and non-compliant listings exit.

Rising slowly through the second half. Anything that depends on a marketplace seller’s take rate in the categories that went up under the new flat-fee structure — accessories at around 12% and home, sport and fashion at around 14% will squeeze sellers who were previously in lower tiers. Expect small, quiet price increases on cheap accessories through August rather than a visible jump.

Flat but worth watching. Supplements. The category is now enormous on short-video commerce and increasingly crowded, which normally means price competition — but the top magnesium product’s commission rate fell from 32% to 25% this month, which is a brand deciding it no longer needs to buy distribution. When commissions fall, retail prices usually follow six to eight weeks later. Wait on supplements.

Bottom Line

Today is a day when the paperwork moved and the prices did not, which is exactly the kind of day that rewards paying attention. The shipping risk on one large marketplace shifted from you to the platform this morning. The return risk on live auctions shifted from the platform to you. A discount ladder opened with a deliberate eight-day gap in the middle designed to hold your attention through the holiday. And on Wednesday, three separate cost changes land at once — one that makes refurbished electronics meaningfully cheaper, and two that make cross-border shopping into Europe and the US structurally more expensive for the first time in a decade.

The playbook for the week is short. Buy the holiday-week gear today, out of a domestic warehouse, before the driving week starts. Buy anything crossing into the EU today, in a single category, before the per-line charge switches on. Wait three days on refurbished electronics. Wait until Friday on the deepest discount tier. And for anything with a battery in it or a child using it, pay the brand premium — the compliance data on the cheap end of that market is genuinely bad, and it is about to be enforced rather than merely true.

The independent-brand list this week is not about aesthetics. It is ten companies that have publicly committed to fixing what they sold you. In a week when three platforms rearranged the liability between buyer and seller, buying from someone who never made you read the fine print in the first place is not sentimental. It is the cheapest form of insurance available.
