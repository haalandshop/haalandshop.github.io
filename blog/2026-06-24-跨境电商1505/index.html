Best Things to Buy Right Now — June 24, 2026

Quick take: Two numbers came out of Prime Day’s first twenty-four hours, and almost every write-up treated them as unrelated. The first: the average order value fell to $48.36, down 17% from $58.37 a year ago. The second: 38% of shoppers said they used an AI tool to help decide what to buy. Those are not two facts. They are one fact, reported twice. When a machine assembles your cart, it does not add the impulse item at the register, it does not get talked into the bigger bundle, and it does not care that the packaging is nice. It buys the thing that satisfies the criteria and it stops. Carts got smaller because carts got more rational. Today, day two of a four-day sale, is a good moment to notice something useful hiding inside that: the checklist an AI agent uses to evaluate a product is the best shopping checklist ever written, and you can just use it yourself. No subscription required.

What’s Hot This Week

The sale, first. Amazon Prime Day runs June 23 through June 26 — four days, starting on a Tuesday, in June rather than July for the second year running. Walmart Deals is in the middle of a seven-day run, June 22 through June 28, no membership required. Target Circle Deal Days overlaps Amazon exactly. Best Buy’s Tech Fest runs alongside Walmart. TikTok Shop’s mid-year promotion has been going since mid-June with platform subsidies. Temu launched a clearance push into the same window. Today is day two of four for Amazon, day three of seven for Walmart. The doorbusters are gone, the first wave of lightning deals has cycled, and what remains is the deep catalog.

What day one actually looked like. Numerator’s live tracker put average order value at $48.36 against $58.37 last year, a 17% drop. Spend per household came in at $89.04 versus $106.41, down 16%. But 59% of households had already placed two or more orders, and 69% of items sold were priced under $20. That is not a collapse in demand. That is a change in shape: more orders, smaller each, concentrated in the cheap end. Apparel and footwear, health and wellness, and household basics led the categories. May inflation ran at 4.2%, which explains part of the trade-down, but not the fragmentation.

Advertising told the other half of the story. CommerceIQ measured return on ad spend at 5.8, up 15% year over year — ads worked better while baskets got smaller, which only happens when the traffic arriving is more decided than it used to be. The same report found that shoppers using Amazon’s Alexa shopping features converted 60% higher than everyone else. Meanwhile, lost sales from out-of-stock hero ASINs jumped 24% year over year even though total inventory was up 12%. Sellers brought more stock and still ran dry on exactly the items people wanted, which is what happens when demand concentrates rather than spreads.

Tinuiti’s study of the event filled in the shopper side: 88% of Prime members planned to shop, up seven points; 55% said price increases were changing their decisions; 59% intended to price-check at Walmart; and 38% used AI tools to help make purchase decisions. The path through this week is no longer “open Amazon, search, buy.” It is closer to read the reviews on Amazon, check the price at Walmart, watch the demo on TikTok, and ask an assistant to settle it.

The bigger thing underneath. A Forrester report published this month found that 23% of US online shoppers have used an AI agent to complete at least one purchase in the past 90 days, up from 6% in the fourth quarter of 2024. That is a near-quadrupling in eighteen months. And these are not browsing sessions. Merchants report that agent-driven sessions which reach checkout complete at rates north of 60%, against a normal human browse-to-buy rate of 2% to 4%. The agent has already done the comparison before it arrives. By the time it lands on a product page, the decision is essentially made and the page is just paperwork.

Shopify’s own numbers point the same direction: AI-driven traffic to its stores is up eightfold year over year since January 2025, and orders originating from AI search are up fifteenfold. At its Spring 2026 edition the company shipped more than 150 updates built around this, the centrepiece being a catalog layer that pushes eligible products into ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and the Shop app automatically, plus a Universal Commerce Protocol developed with Google that standardizes discovery, cart and checkout for agents. Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce, Stripe, Etsy, Target and Wayfair have all signed on to it. Shopify reports that AI search conversion against a structured catalog runs twice what it gets from agents scraping storefront HTML.

And the part everyone skips. Trust has not kept pace. European research this month put the ten-year opportunity for agent-driven shopping at €310 billion — while finding that only 41% of consumers trust AI shopping services at all. The breakdown by category is the interesting bit: 45% are comfortable letting an agent buy electronics, and only 16% are comfortable letting it buy groceries. People have already worked out, without being told, exactly where the technology is good and where it is not. Electronics are specifications. Groceries are ripeness, and preference, and the specific brand of oat milk that does not curdle. Nobody needs a research report to feel that difference.

Which brings us to the strategy for this week. Let the checklist do the commodities. Spend your own judgment where the checklist runs out. The rest of this piece is organized around that split.

Platform & Category Watch

Amazon. ZDNET’s tracking of what its readers actually bought on day two produced a chart dominated by one brand: Garmin took four of the top ten slots, including the Fenix 8 Pro at $950 after a $350 cut and the Epix Pro Gen 2 Sapphire at $500, a straight 50% off. Below that sat the $13 Amazon Smart Plug, RayNeo Air 4 Pro display glasses at $239 after $60 off, and a Scosche magnetic car charger at $30. A Pokemon Mega Evolution training box at $99 was the single best seller and sold out. Note the shape of that list: it is almost entirely products defined by numbers you can look up, plus one collectible that is defined by scarcity. There is very little in the middle.

Independent purchase-panel data from Subtotal, covering real linked Amazon accounts over the June 23 to 26 window against a June 16 to 19 baseline, shows the everyday layer beneath the headline deals: Stanley IceFlow tumblers, organic applesauce pouches, Lavazza Super Crema whole bean coffee, canned iced tea, energy drink mix, and dish spray were the highest-unit items. The most expensive things people actually bought were an Acer Nitro 16 gaming laptop at $1,880, a 15-inch MacBook Air at $1,550, a 45-inch OLED gaming monitor at $1,338, a 32TB NAS drive at $1,160, a robot vacuum at $885 and an espresso machine at $750. Cheap consumables at the top by volume, a handful of considered purchases at the bottom by value, and a hollowed-out middle. That is the trade-down pattern in one table.

Elsewhere in the deal mix: Oura, Sony and Lenovo all had lines marked more than 50% off, World Cup merchandise is moving unusually well alongside detergent and trash bags, and Amazon has confirmed Prime Big Deal Days for October 8 — worth knowing now if you are deciding whether something can wait.

Walmart. No membership gate, seven days, and an active real-time price-match posture against Amazon. With 59% of Prime Day shoppers saying they intend to comparison-shop here, Walmart’s role this week is less “alternative retailer” and more “the number Amazon is being measured against.” Practically, that means the gap between the two on any given commodity item is unusually small right now, and the deciding factor is more likely to be delivery timing than price.

Target. Circle Deal Days matches Amazon’s dates. The important detail is that the basic Circle tier costs nothing, so the event pricing is effectively open. Free same-day delivery kicks in over a $35 basket, which is easy to clear if you are buying consumables anyway.

TikTok Shop. The June top-ten list came in at roughly $25.8 million in combined revenue against $22.7 million in May, with six of ten positions turning over — a churn rate that tells you the chart is being driven by discovery rather than by established demand. The category mix on the US side is heavily concentrated: across the top thousand products, beauty and personal care takes about 30%, health and wellness close to 20%, and apparel and accessories 17%. Two thirds of revenue on the platform is things you put on or in your body. Home goods is the fastest-growing block at roughly 15% of GMV.

One number from that chart is worth carrying with you as a general-purpose warning sign. The product at number eight had the highest average selling price on the entire chart at $558.75 and the lowest creator conversion rate at 18.06%. High revenue, low conversion, premium price. That combination does not describe a product people want. It describes a product a lot of money is being spent to promote. When you see something everywhere at once and the price is at the top of its category, that is the pattern to check for.

Independent sites and the agent layer. For anyone shopping direct-to-consumer this week, the practical development is that the platforms are actively splitting into machine-readable and machine-invisible. BigCommerce launched an “agent ready” certification in May with Perplexity and Anthropic. Shopify’s catalog is on by default for eligible products. WooCommerce is relying on its plugin ecosystem and has structured data on the 9.2 roadmap. The gap this creates is going to be visible to you as a shopper long before it is visible in any market share report: some brand sites will answer a specific question in one click, and some will make you read a paragraph of adjectives to find out how much something weighs.

Logistics and policy, briefly, because it hits prices later. The Strait of Hormuz reopened following a US-Iran agreement, and oil fell hard — New York light crude to $80.25 a barrel, down 5.45%, and Brent to $83.51, down 4.37%. Fuel surcharges follow crude with a lag of roughly a quarter, so this is a Q4 price story rather than a today story. MSC has warned that the global container orderbook now sits at about 30% of the existing fleet with most deliveries landing around 2027, which points to overcapacity and softer freight rates in the medium term. Against that, several changes land on July 1: the EU removes its €22 duty-free threshold and applies a flat €3 charge to small parcels, eBay requires delivered-duty-paid shipping on anything under €150 going from mainland China or Hong Kong into the EU, and Ozon collapses its commission tiers from six to three. US Customs also revoked more than 800 broker licences for missed filings, which will slow some clearances. If you are buying anything that ships internationally, the cheap window is now and the friction arrives next week.

The EU rule that is already live. Directive 2023/2673 became mandatory on June 19. Any distance contract sold to an EU consumer — physical goods, digital content, subscriptions, on any platform or independent site — must now carry a one-click withdrawal function. The button has to be visible throughout, worded precisely, and reachable in no more than two steps. The seller must send a timestamped confirmation automatically and keep records for twelve months. Non-compliance carries fines up to 4% of global annual turnover, and, more usefully for you, automatically extends the cooling-off period to twelve months and fourteen days. If you are shopping into or inside the EU this week and a store has not built the button, your right to unwind the purchase runs for over a year instead of a fortnight.

Top Marketplace Picks

Ten things worth buying on the big platforms right now. Every one of them was chosen on the same basis: the important facts about it are published numbers rather than published adjectives.

1. Garmin Epix Pro Gen 2 Sapphire — Amazon. Fifty percent off, $500 from $1,000, and the single most interesting deal of the event. This is a previous-generation flagship being cleared while the Fenix 8 Pro sits above it at $950. You are paying half price for a sapphire crystal, a titanium bezel, a flashlight, multi-band GPS and a battery measured in weeks. The current-generation advantages are real but incremental, and they are not $450 of incremental. Last-generation flagship at half price is the most reliably underpriced category in consumer electronics, and it is underpriced precisely because it does not photograph as well as “new.” Search on Amazon

2. Oura Ring — Amazon. Marked more than 50% off in this event, and worth understanding before you buy: the hardware is a one-time purchase but the full feature set sits behind a subscription of roughly $6 a month. That is not a reason to avoid it — it is a reason to do the arithmetic over three years rather than looking at the sticker. At half off the hardware, the three-year total lands well below where it normally sits. Check ring sizing carefully, because the sizing kit step is the most common source of returns in this category. Search on Amazon

3. Seagate IronWolf 32TB NAS Drive — Amazon. The purest example on this list of a product with nothing to sell you but numbers. Capacity, sustained transfer rate, workload rating in terabytes per year, mean time between failures, warranty length, cache size, and whether it uses conventional or shingled recording — every one of those is published, comparable, and decisive. There is no brand story here and there does not need to be one. If you run a home server or a serious media archive, this is the week to buy storage, because storage discounting during a sale event is unusually honest: nobody can fake a spec sheet. Confirm CMR rather than SMR before you buy if the drive is going into a RAID array. Search on Amazon

4. RayNeo Air 4 Pro Display Glasses — Amazon. $239 after $60 off, and a genuinely useful category that is finally cheap enough to experiment with. These are not augmented reality in the ambitious sense — they are a large virtual display strapped to your face for watching things on a plane or working from a laptop in a cramped seat. Buy on the published numbers: panel type, resolution per eye, field of view in degrees, brightness in nits, weight in grams, and whether dioptre adjustment is built in or requires prescription inserts. Weight is the spec that determines whether you keep using them after month one. Search on Amazon

5. Amazon Smart Plug — Amazon. Thirteen dollars, down from $25. This is the least glamorous item on the list and probably the highest ratio of usefulness to cost. A smart plug converts any dumb appliance — a fan, a lamp, a coffee machine, a Christmas tree in six months — into a scheduled one. The reason to buy the first-party version rather than a cheaper generic is that setup is genuinely one step if you already have an Echo, and the failure mode of cheap smart plugs is a cloud service that quietly shuts down. Check the amperage rating against whatever you intend to plug into it; most are rated well below what a space heater draws. Search on Amazon

6. Celsius Energy Drink Mix — Walmart. One of the highest-unit items in independent panel data this week, and a good demonstration of why Walmart matters right now. This is a pure replenishment purchase — you know if you want it, there is no research to do, and the only variable is price per serving. With Walmart price-matching in real time and no membership gate, this is exactly the kind of item where the platform difference is the whole decision. Buy on cost per stick and check the caffeine milligrams against what you actually want, because the range across variants in this category is wide. Search on Walmart

7. Lenovo Laptop — Walmart. Lenovo is running more than 50% off on selected lines this week. Laptops are the category where the specification-versus-marketing gap is at its widest, so buy strictly on the published grid: exact processor model number rather than the family name, RAM amount and whether it is soldered or upgradeable, storage type and interface, screen resolution and nits of brightness, battery capacity in watt-hours, and port list. The single most common regret in a discounted laptop is soldered RAM at 8GB, which is not visible in the headline and cannot be fixed later. Search on Walmart

8. Olaplex No. 9 Bond Protector — Target. Running 40% off as a timed deal this week. Haircare is a category where ingredient lists do most of the honest work and the marketing does the rest, so the useful move is to check the concentration and position of the active ingredients rather than the claims on the front. Target is the right venue for this one specifically because the basic Circle tier is free, the event pricing is open to everyone, and stacking it with other consumables clears the $35 threshold for free same-day delivery. 9. Dr.Melaxin Gifted Collagen Boost Set — TikTok Shop. This one is on the list for a slightly contrarian reason. It placed seventh on TikTok’s June chart at $2.01 million, down from $2.64 million in May and $3.12 million in April — three straight months of decline, growth now negative at -2.6%. But over the same period its creator conversion rate rose from 25.42% to 32.25% while creator count grew from 2,010 to 2,810. Falling revenue with rising conversion is the signature of a product moving past its hype cycle into its actual audience. The people still buying it are the people it works for. That is usually the best moment to buy something, and the worst moment to be selling it. Browse TikTok Shop

10. Pokemon TCG Mega Evolution Training Box — eBay. The best-selling item in day-two tracking, and out of stock on Amazon, which is precisely why it belongs here. When a sealed collectible sells out during the largest sale of the year, the aftermarket becomes the market. eBay is the honest venue for this: sold listings give you a real transaction history rather than an asking price, which is the one thing you cannot get anywhere else. Filter to completed sales before you decide what it is worth, check the seller’s feedback on sealed product specifically, and do not pay a scarcity premium above roughly 130% of the $99 retail unless you are buying it to open rather than to hold. Best Direct-to-Consumer & Brand Picks

Every brand below was chosen on one criterion: their product pages publish hard, checkable numbers. That is the same thing an AI agent looks for, and it is not a coincidence — a company willing to put the gram weight, the certification, the failure rating and the return terms in plain text is a company whose product survives being looked at closely.

1. Traeger — pellet grills. Published cooking area in square inches, temperature range in degrees, pellet hopper capacity in pounds, and controller precision. Grills are a category built on outdoor lifestyle photography, and Traeger’s spec transparency lets you ignore all of it. Buy on total cooking square inches against how many people you actually feed, and check the temperature floor rather than the ceiling — low-and-slow capability is what separates a smoker from a hot metal box. Visit Traeger

2. iRobot — robot vacuums. Runtime in minutes, suction in pascals, bin capacity in millilitres, and whether the self-empty base is included or sold separately. This is a category where the meaningful differences are entirely in published numbers and the meaningless differences are entirely in the naming convention. Buy on runtime against your floor area first, then on whether it maps, then on bin size. Note that the self-empty base is frequently the difference between a machine you use and one you resent. Visit iRobot

3. Milwaukee — power tools. Amp-hours on the battery, torque in inch-pounds or newton-metres, weight in pounds, and a warranty measured in years rather than adjectives. Power tools are the original spec-sheet category and Milwaukee’s numbers are unusually honest about the trade-off between battery capacity and tool weight. Buy into one battery platform and stay in it — the batteries, not the tools, are where the money goes over five years. Visit Milwaukee Tool

4. Organic Basics — everyday apparel. GOTS certification, fabric composition by percentage, and named factory information. Basics are the single most commoditized apparel category and therefore the one where brand storytelling does the most work relative to product difference. Certification is the antidote: it is either present or it is not. Buy on fibre composition and GSM weight, and treat everything else in the listing as decoration. Visit Organic Basics

5. Reformation — womenswear. Publishes fabric composition alongside a per-garment environmental scorecard with water and carbon figures. Whatever you make of the sustainability framing, the practical effect is that you can see exactly what a garment is made of before you buy, which in this price bracket is not universal. Buy on composition and check the care instructions before you commit — the difference between machine-washable and dry-clean-only is a real ongoing cost that nobody prices in at checkout. Visit Reformation

6. Roka — eyewear. Lens specifications, frame weight in grams, and published fit geometry. Sunglasses are sold almost entirely on how they look on somebody else’s face, so a brand that leads with the weight and the optical spec is doing something unusual. Weight is the number that determines whether you wear them for a full day. Buy on grams and lens category rating, then worry about the shape. Visit Roka

7. Birkenstock — footwear. Footbed construction, cork and latex composition, and consistent EU sizing with a published width standard. This is a brand whose entire proposition is a technical specification that has not changed much in decades, which makes it the easiest footwear purchase to research. Get the width right — regular versus narrow is the source of nearly every complaint about this product — and buy the footbed material that suits your climate. Visit Birkenstock

8. ELITEWHEELS — cycling wheels. Rim weight in grams, depth in millimetres, internal width, spoke count and rider weight limit. Carbon wheels are a category with an enormous gap between price and performance, and the only way through it is the published number. Buy on rim depth matched to your terrain rather than to what looks fast, and check the rider weight limit against your actual loaded weight, which people routinely underestimate. Visit ELITEWHEELS

9. LifeStraw — water filtration. Litres filtered before replacement, micron rating, and named contaminant removal percentages tested against a published standard. This is possibly the cleanest example on the list of a product where the claim is verifiable and the verification is the product. Buy on the contaminant list matched to where you are actually going, not on total capacity — the capacity number is easy to hit and the contaminant spec is where the differences live. Visit LifeStraw

10. Veja — sneakers. Material composition published by percentage, with sourcing detail down to the specific input. Sneakers are the category most thoroughly dominated by marketing spend, so the ability to read what a shoe is actually made of before buying is genuinely rare. Buy on the upper material against your climate — the range across their line runs from leather to canvas to recycled synthetics, and they perform very differently in rain. Note that sizing on this brand runs consistently large, which is documented but easy to miss. Visit Veja

Buying Tips for This Week

Use the agent checklist even if you never use an agent. The signals that AI shopping tools weight most heavily, according to platform documentation, are price, availability, brand, GTIN and return policy — specifically when those are present as structured, unambiguous data. You can apply this by hand in about fifteen seconds. Open the product page. Can you find the exact weight, the exact dimensions, the exact material, the exact warranty length and the exact return window without scrolling past a lifestyle photograph? If yes, the seller is confident. If the page instead offers “premium,” “upgraded,” “professional grade” and “2026 new version,” the seller is managing your expectations rather than meeting them. This test costs nothing and it is remarkably reliable.

Read the returns page before the product page. There is a documented gap in how sellers write these. “Free returns within 30 days, no restocking fee, prepaid label included” is a policy. “Hassle-free returns” is a mood. Agents parse the first and choke on the second, and so should you, because the vagueness is usually load-bearing. During a four-day sale with compressed decision time, the return window is the actual product guarantee.

In the EU, check for the withdrawal button. As of June 19, any store selling to EU consumers must offer one-click withdrawal in no more than two steps, with an automatic timestamped confirmation. If the store you are buying from has not implemented it, your cooling-off period automatically extends to twelve months and fourteen days rather than the standard fourteen days. That is a substantial consumer protection and very few shoppers know it exists yet.

Watch for the promotion signature. Highest price in its category plus lowest conversion rate plus sudden ubiquity equals a marketing budget, not a consensus. You cannot see conversion rates directly, but you can see the proxies: an item appearing in an unusual number of creator videos in a short window, all posted within days of each other, all using similar phrasing. Genuine word of mouth is staggered and inconsistent. Campaigns are synchronized.

Beware the physically ambitious novelty. A useful live example this week: a floating pool umbrella combining shade, cup holders, phone storage and a floating table has been doing around $97,000 in weekly TikTok Shop revenue at roughly $190. The concept is good. The reviews concentrate on durability of the floating materials and the connection points — which is to say, the parts that have to survive being in water and being sat on. Novelty products fail at their joints. Before buying anything in this class, read the one-star and two-star reviews first and specifically look for the word “seam,” “joint,” “hinge” or “connector.”

Know the real markup on simple objects. A magnetic foldable microwave splatter guard is currently selling well at around $20, and equivalent product is available from suppliers at under $3. That is not fraud — somebody has to design, warehouse, ship and support it — but it does tell you the category has enormous pricing latitude, which means the discount you are offered can be deep and still profitable. For simple moulded or stamped goods, being patient for a real discount costs you nothing.

Buy the last generation. This week’s clearest illustration is a flagship watch at 50% off while its successor sits at a $350 discount from a much higher base. In electronics, generational improvements are real but they compound slowly, while the price gap between current and previous generation opens sharply during sale events. If you can name the specific feature the new generation added and you actually need it, buy new. If you cannot name it, you are buying the year on the box.

Time the international purchase. Oil dropped sharply this week and freight capacity is heading toward oversupply around 2027, both of which are good news for landed costs eventually. But on July 1 the EU’s €22 duty-free threshold disappears and a flat €3 charge applies to small parcels, delivered-duty-paid becomes mandatory on low-value shipments from mainland China and Hong Kong into the EU on eBay, and several platforms adjust fees. If you are buying cross-border this week, you are buying inside the last cheap window before the friction arrives.

Do not let the machine buy your groceries. Only 16% of consumers are comfortable with that, against 45% for electronics, and the instinct is correct. Categories where agents genuinely outperform you are the ones with quantifiable criteria and low brand attachment: electronics and accessories, storage, batteries, consumable replenishment of things you have already chosen. Categories where they underperform are the ones involving fit, ripeness, visual judgment and room context — apparel beyond basics, furniture, produce, anything where “correct” depends on you. Use the tool inside its competence and no further.

What’s Coming Next

This week finishes fast. Amazon and Target close on June 26, Walmart and Best Buy on June 28. Historically the final day of a multi-day event carries the deepest clearance on overstocked lines and the thinnest availability on anything that was genuinely popular. Given that out-of-stock losses on hero items are already running 24% above last year, the calculation is straightforward: if the item you want is specific and popular, buy it today. If you are flexible and hunting for value, wait for the last day.

Then the calendar goes quiet, deliberately. Amazon has confirmed Prime Big Deal Days for October 8, which is the next comparable event on the US calendar. That is a fifteen-week gap. Anything you defer today, you are realistically deferring into October unless it goes on independent promotion. For durable goods that is often a fine trade. For anything you will use through summer, it is not.

July 1 is the friction date. The EU’s €22 duty-free threshold ends and a flat €3 charge applies to small parcels. eBay mandates delivered-duty-paid on sub-€150 shipments from mainland China and Hong Kong into the EU. Ozon compresses its commission structure from six price bands to three, raising rates on higher-value items — which will show up as retail price increases on Russian marketplace listings within weeks. FedEx has already issued guidance to shippers. If your purchase crosses a border, the cost of crossing it goes up next Wednesday.

Amazon’s seller-side rules tighten through the summer. Merchant-fulfilled handling time verification starts June 29, with the platform comparing stated processing times against thirty days of actual performance and overriding sellers who inflate their windows. From July 31, the AWD warehouse network stops accepting oversized items. Neither is aimed at shoppers, but both push in the same direction: stated delivery dates on marketplace listings should become more accurate through Q3, and oversized goods may see availability shifts as inventory reroutes.

Southeast Asia moves into its own season. Shopee’s 9.9 event is already in pre-promotion, and Vietnam has launched an affiliate marketing programme that will widen the creator-driven discovery channel there considerably. If you buy from that region, the promotional density increases from here through September.

The agent layer keeps consolidating. Shopify’s structured catalog is expected to reach all plan tiers by the fourth quarter. BigCommerce’s certification programme is live. WooCommerce’s structured data work is on the 9.2 roadmap. By the holiday season, the practical difference between a store an assistant can read and one it cannot will be visible in whether that store shows up at all when someone asks a chatbot for a recommendation. As a shopper this mostly benefits you — but it also means the stores you find through AI will increasingly be the ones that invested in being findable, which is not the same thing as the ones with the best products. Keep a manual search in your routine.

Price Trend Outlook

Consumables: flat to slightly up. With 69% of Prime Day items selling under $20 and household basics leading the category mix, the low end is where competitive pressure is most intense and margins are thinnest. Sellers cannot go much lower. Against 4.2% inflation, expect nominal prices to drift up gently through the second half while promotional depth on individual items stays aggressive. The strategy that works here is stocking up on the specific things you actually repurchase during events, rather than hunting for a structurally lower price that is not coming.

Consumer electronics: down, unevenly. This is the clearest opportunity in the market right now, and agentic shopping is part of why. When purchase decisions are made on published specifications rather than brand impression, previous-generation hardware stops carrying a stigma and starts trading closer to its actual utility. A half-price previous-generation flagship is not an anomaly this week — it is what happens when the market gets better at reading spec sheets. Expect that discount pattern to deepen through the autumn as agent-mediated comparison spreads. Storage, displays and accessories should soften fastest because they are the purest spec plays.

Apparel and footwear: volume up, value down. Apparel led Prime Day’s category mix while average order value fell 17%, which means units are moving and prices are not holding. Basics are the most exposed, since they are the part of the category agents can already handle competently. Expect continued discounting on commodity apparel and continued resilience at the branded and fit-dependent end, where the machine cannot help and the shopper still has to care.

Beauty and personal care: bifurcating. This is roughly 30% of TikTok Shop’s top-thousand mix and it is splitting cleanly. Ingredient-led products with published formulations are holding price because the claim is checkable. Products sold on packaging and creator enthusiasm are seeing sharp revenue declines once the initial wave passes — as visible in a top-ten product losing a third of its revenue over three months while its conversion rate climbed. Buy the formulation, expect the marketing-led items to be cheaper in ninety days.

Home goods: rising volume, falling average price. The fastest-growing block on TikTok Shop at around 15% of GMV, and mostly composed of inexpensive problem-solvers. High growth plus low barriers to entry plus enormous supplier margin latitude equals price compression. Anything in this category that is simple to manufacture will be cheaper in six months. Anything that requires real engineering — appliances with motors, compressors or heating elements — will not.

Cross-border landed costs: down later, up sooner. Crude at $80.25 with the Hormuz route reopened removes a significant risk premium, and MSC’s warning about a container orderbook at 30% of existing fleet points to genuine overcapacity by 2027. Both are deflationary for shipping. But that is a 2027 story arriving through a 2026 that includes the EU’s small-parcel charges from July 1, mandatory DDP on low-value shipments, and slower customs clearance following the revocation of 800-plus broker licences. Near-term costs go up. Medium-term costs come down. Buy cross-border now, not in August.

The structural shift worth watching. Agent-driven purchasing went from 6% to 23% of US online shoppers in eighteen months, and the sessions convert at over 60%. If that continues, the long-run effect on prices is compression in every category where the criteria are quantifiable — because a machine comparing forty options on published numbers will find the efficient price every single time, and it is immune to the packaging. The categories that will hold their pricing are the ones where the criteria are not quantifiable: fit, taste, aesthetics, room context, and genuine brand attachment. That is a useful map of where your money is well spent and where it is merely being persuaded.

Bottom Line

Prime Day day two is a good vantage point because the theatre has worn off and what is left is the arithmetic. The average order fell 17% while advertising efficiency rose 15%, and 38% of shoppers had a machine helping them decide. Those three numbers describe a market that is getting harder to sell to and easier to buy in.

The advice that follows from it is not complicated. Shop like the agent: demand the number, check the return policy, ignore the adjective. A product page that tells you the grams, the watts, the micron rating, the certification and the exact return window is a product page whose owner is not worried about you looking closely. A page that leads with a photograph and a founder story might still be selling something excellent, but you will have to find that out some other way.

And then stop where the agent stops. Twenty-three percent of American shoppers have now let software complete a purchase for them, and they are overwhelmingly doing it for electronics, storage, batteries and repeat-buy consumables — the things where being right is a matter of specification. Only 16% will let it buy groceries. That instinct is not technophobia. It is an accurate assessment of where judgment still belongs to you.

This week, let the checklist handle the commodities. Buy the previous-generation flagship at half price. Buy the storage on the spec sheet. Buy the consumables you were going to buy anyway, at Walmart, where the price is being disciplined in real time. Then take the money and the attention you saved, and spend both on the handful of things where fit, feel and taste actually matter — because those are the purchases no machine is going to get right for you, this year or any year.
