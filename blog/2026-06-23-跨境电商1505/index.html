Best Things to Buy Right Now — June 23, 2026

Quick take: Two facts landed within hours of each other today, and most coverage filed them under opposite headlines. The first: US shoppers spent $8.3 billion online, the single biggest e-commerce day of 2026 so far. The second: the average order size fell to $48.36, down 17% from a year ago, and roughly 69% of purchases came in under $20. The obvious reading is that people are buying more cheap stuff. That reading is half right, and the half it misses is the useful half. Because at the same moment the average basket shrank, the share of the most expensive goods sold rose 19% across every tracked category — and in electronics it rose 48%. People are not trading down. They are trading out of the middle. They buy the cheapest acceptable version of things they do not care about, and the best available version of things they do. The $60-to-$150 “pretty good” product is the thing dying on day one of Prime Day, and it is also the thing most of this week’s fake discounts are attached to. Shop the two ends. Skip the middle.

What’s Hot This Week

The calendar, first, because four sales are running at once and they do not end together. Amazon Prime Day runs June 23 through June 26 — four days, opening on a Tuesday, in June rather than July for only the second time ever. The first June edition was 2021. This year’s date was not a whim: the traditional July window now collides with both the FIFA World Cup and the run-up to Independence Day, two events that compete hard for attention and ad inventory, so Amazon moved rather than fought. Walmart Deals is already on day two of a seven-day run that started June 22 and finishes June 28, with no membership gate at all. Target Circle Deal Days mirrors Amazon’s window almost exactly. Best Buy’s Tech Fest is running alongside. Today is opening day for Amazon and Target, mid-early for Walmart, and that asymmetry matters more than it sounds — the Walmart event outlives the Amazon one by two full days, which is where the genuinely desperate markdowns tend to appear.

The record, and the number underneath it. US retailers drove $8.3 billion in online spend today, up 5.3% from the $7.9 billion recorded on the opening day of last year’s event. That makes today the largest single e-commerce day anywhere in the US in 2026, and it beat the pre-event projection rather than merely meeting it. For scale: American shoppers spent $6.4 billion online on Thanksgiving 2025, $11.8 billion on Black Friday, and $14.2 billion on Cyber Monday. The four-day forecast for this event sits at $26.3 billion, about 9% growth, which would be more than Black Friday and Cyber Monday combined. Summer has quietly stopped being the off-season. JPMorgan’s read is that the event pulls $7 billion to $8 billion of incremental global revenue into Amazon’s second quarter.

Now the contradiction. Independent household panel tracking for the same twenty-four hours found average order size at $48.36, down roughly 17% from $58.37 last year. Average household spend came in at $89.04, down 16% from $106.41. The average price paid per individual item fell about 9%, to $23.07. Nearly seven in ten purchases were under $20. Every one of those arrows points down, on a day the aggregate hit an all-time high.

The standard explanation is trade-down: inflation bites, people buy cheaper things, totals hold up because more people shop. That explanation is incomplete, and you can prove it from the same dataset. Across all tracked categories, the share of the most expensive goods sold rose 19% relative to the year-to-date average. In electronics that share rose 48%. In toys it rose 37%. In appliances it rose 30%. Those are not trade-down numbers. Those are trade-up numbers, and they are happening simultaneously with a 17% drop in average order value.

This is a barbell, and it is the most useful thing to understand about shopping in 2026. Both ends of the price ladder are growing. The bottom is growing in volume: more orders, more items, more sub-$20 purchases, heavy concentration in consumables and replenishment. The top is growing in mix: when somebody does make a considered purchase, they are markedly more likely to buy the premium version than they were a year ago. What is contracting is everything between. The mid-tier product — the one that is not cheap enough to be thoughtless and not good enough to be permanent — is losing on both flanks at once.

Two categories confirm it from opposite directions. Home and garden saw the share of most-expensive goods fall 5%, and grocery fell 2%. Those are the categories where people genuinely did trade down, and they are exactly the categories where the product is functionally interchangeable. Meanwhile electronics, where the difference between the good one and the mediocre one is legible and permanent, went the other way by 48 points.

The other number worth sitting with: promotional fatigue is now measurable. Only 41% of shoppers said the event itself was the main reason they were shopping, down from 52% a year ago. That is an eleven-point collapse in a single year, and it is the quiet story of this Prime Day. When barely two in five participants are there because of the sale, the sale has stopped being an event and started being a background condition. The practical consequence for you is straightforward: the urgency is manufactured, the four-day format explicitly gives you room to wait, and the people who shopped today at 3am got very little that the people shopping Thursday afternoon will not also get.

Platform & Category Watch

Where the demand actually went on day one. Measured against average daily sales in June, electronics finished up 105% and appliances up 95%. Tools and home improvement rose 75%, home and garden 65%. Those are the expected big-ticket winners. The interesting part is what sat alongside them: personal hygiene products — oral care, shampoo, antiperspirant — were up 130%, and household goods like trash bags, detergents and paper products were up 65%. Those are pure replenishment categories with no research component whatsoever, and they beat several of the glamour categories.

Baby products produced the single steepest jumps of the day. Strollers were up 220%. Car seats up 140%. Formula up 90%, diapers and wipes up 85%. School supplies climbed 140%, smart watches 130%, exercise equipment 125%. Read that list as a barbell and it snaps into focus: a stroller is a considered, expensive, safety-rated purchase where nobody buys the third-best option, and diapers are the most commoditized recurring purchase in the house. Both spiked. The mid-priced nursery decor did not.

The discount depth chart is the most actionable table nobody reads. Discounts across the event are landing in a 10% to 24% band. Electronics is deepest at roughly 24%, apparel just behind at 23%, toys at 18%, televisions at 17%, appliances at 17%, furniture at 16%, computers at 15%, and sporting goods dead last at 9%.

That ordering is a shopping list in disguise. Electronics and apparel are where the money actually comes off. Sporting goods at 9% is barely a discount at all — that category simply is not participating this year, and anything you see marked in it is close to a rounding error against normal pricing. Computers at 15% is the trap of the week: it is the category people most expect to be cheap during a summer event, and it is running two-thirds the depth of general electronics. If you want a laptop, the deal is usually a specific model being cleared, not the category being discounted.

Mobile crossed a threshold today. Phones drove 51.2% of online sales, an all-time high for a Prime Day opening day, and $4.24 billion in spend. That is the first time a majority of opening-day dollars have arrived from a handset. Buy-now-pay-later accounted for $668 million, 6.5% of orders, up 7.6% year over year. Neither number is shocking on its own; together they describe a shopper standing in a kitchen, deciding in ninety seconds, and splitting the cost. That is not a person who comparison-shopped for a week.

The influence numbers deserve more attention than they get. Social media drove just 4.3% of revenue, but it was the fastest-growing source, up 34.1% year over year. Affiliates and partners — which includes influencer content — took 21.7% of revenue share. The gap between those two is the whole lesson: influencers converted shoppers twelve times more than social networks did overall. Affiliate and partner traffic hit a 9% add-to-cart rate and a 57% checkout-initiation rate, against 3% and 27% for social broadly. Social is where you hear about something. Affiliate is where you buy it. Treating them as one channel is how people mis-read their own shopping behaviour.

And the single biggest reversal of the year. During last year’s Prime Day, traffic arriving from AI assistants converted 23% worse than every other channel. Today, that same traffic converted 50.7% better. Volume nearly doubled year over year. AI-referred shoppers spent 49.9% longer on site, viewed 20.5% more pages per visit, and added to cart at a 33% higher rate.

A seventy-point swing in twelve months is not a gradual improvement, it is a change in what the traffic is. Last year, asking an assistant what to buy was a browsing behaviour — curiosity, tire-kicking, a fancier way of reading reviews. This year, people arrive having already made the decision elsewhere and use the assistant to execute it. The counterweight: as much as 46% of retail sites remain effectively unreadable by machines. Half the internet is now invisible in the place where a growing share of buying decisions get finalized, and the merchants on the wrong side of that line do not know it yet.

Platform side, the infrastructure moved fast this month. Shopify shipped its Spring 2026 Edition on June 17 — over 150 updates in a single drop, the largest release the company has done. The centre of it is the Universal Commerce Protocol, co-developed with Google and switched on by default for every store, which automatically syndicates eligible products to ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode, Gemini and the Shop app with no merchant setup. Shopify Catalog does the same job for structured product data at the scale of billions of items. A new Agentic section in every admin reports which AI channels a product surfaces in. Shop Pay now runs on stores that are not on Shopify at all, reaching a base of 250 million-plus shoppers. Checkout Components went generally available for Plus merchants with early adopters reporting 8% to 22% conversion lifts. The financial backdrop: first-quarter GMV crossed $100.7 billion, up 35%, on revenue of $3.17 billion, up 34%, with AI-driven store traffic up eightfold. One hard deadline for anyone running a store — Shopify Scripts stops executing June 30, and anything still depending on it simply breaks.

Visa and OpenAI announced payment infrastructure for AI agents on June 17, covering tokenization, authorization and fraud monitoring. Separate research puts roughly 14% of online purchase sessions on major English-language storefronts as involving some agentic AI interaction, against under 3% in January 2025.

TikTok Shop’s June chart turned over hard. Combined revenue for the US top ten climbed to about $25.8 million from roughly $22.7 million in May, with six of the ten positions changing hands. Ninja took two simultaneous slots for the first time, with the SLUSHi frozen-drink machine debuting at $1.95 million and the CREAMi XL Deluxe at $2.18 million. The quarter’s breakout list is more interesting than the top ten: a fascia massager posted $3.88 million in June on 1,539% growth, a portable power station did $4.53 million on 247%, and a $15.98 beach graphic tee cleared $7.88 million across the quarter — the only apparel item in the entire ranking and the cheapest thing on it. Category mix across the US top thousand runs roughly beauty and personal care 30%, health 20%, apparel 17%, with home goods the fastest-growing block at around 15% of GMV.

On the collectibles side, an eBay trend report covering the US, UK, German and Australian sites for January through May found something counter-intuitive: in remote-control toys, products above $300 account for more than 40% of the top-listing chart, and RC posts the highest conversion rate of any collectible toy category. Display cases and cabinets for die-cast cars broke into the top twenty as a category in their own right. Model paints make up over 55% of the modelling category’s top listings. Hydraulic engineering RC — excavators and loaders with working hydraulics — is emerging as a genuinely high-ticket niche with average order values above $200. Licensed IP drives both traffic and conversion across all four categories. That is a market behaving like the barbell too: cheap consumable paints at one end, $300-plus licensed machines at the other.

Cross-border policy had a loud day. The UK Treasury confirmed this morning that it is pulling forward the removal of the £135 low-value import duty exemption by six months, to October 2028 rather than March 2029. Low-value imports into the UK went from £3.8 billion in 2023-24 to £5.9 billion in 2024-25, growth of about 53%, with China-origin parcels making up roughly 51% of the total. Sixteen major retailers including Marks & Spencer, Next and Primark wrote to the Treasury in May demanding faster action; the industry response to today’s announcement was that six months is nowhere near enough. The Treasury separately said it will review how VAT is collected on online marketplaces.

That sits inside a wider squeeze. The EU removes its €150 low-value exemption on July 1, replacing it with a flat €3 per-parcel duty — Amazon has already notified sellers that both FBA and FBM orders shipped from outside the EU will carry it, that FBM sellers must use designated carriers and supply a valid IOSS number, and that Pan-European and EFN inventory will have the €3 plus VAT added automatically to the displayed price. The Netherlands, meanwhile, formally abandoned its own €2 parcel handling fee today, choosing to wait for the EU-level regime instead; France remains the only country with a national parcel tax actually in force. The US closed its own de minimis channel in 2025.

Elsewhere: Indonesia’s Tanjung Priok port is sitting on a backlog of 2,500 to 3,000 customs declarations with roughly 3,100 containers stuck, and the finance ministry has ordered 24-hour customs shifts. A ministerial regulation effective June 17 requires Shopee, TikTok Shop and Lazada to cut platform fees by at least 50% for certified local micro and small sellers. Lazada’s 6.6 event delivered a 276% GMV jump in Vietnam with average order value up 61% and a 42.5% repurchase rate; the Philippines site was up 260%. TikTok Shop Mexico’s Hot Sale sales rose more than eightfold year over year. Russian desktop PC sales fell 31% by volume in the first quarter, the sharpest decline since the early 2020s, as component shortages pushed system prices up 1.5 to 2 times.

Top Marketplace Picks

Ten things worth buying across the big platforms today. Every one was selected against the barbell: it is either genuinely at the cheap end and worth having anyway, or it is at the premium end where this week’s discount curve is actually steep. Nothing here lives in the $60-to-$150 dead zone.

1. De’Longhi La Specialista Arte Evo Espresso Machine — Amazon. Forty-three percent off, and the clearest example on this list of why the top of the ladder is where the money comes off. Appliances ran up 95% against normal June volume today, and the discount band for the category sits around 17% — this one is running more than double that, which means it is a specific unit being cleared rather than a category-wide gesture. Buy it on the published mechanicals: bar pressure at the group head rather than the pump, whether the grinder is conical burr or blade, portafilter diameter in millimetres, and boiler type. The 58mm portafilter is the spec that matters most long-term, because it is the size the entire aftermarket is built around. Search on Amazon

2. Owlet Dream Duo — Amazon. Up to 33% off, and it sits inside the loudest category signal of the day. Baby products produced the steepest jumps on the board — strollers up 220%, car seats up 140% — because nobody shops the middle of that ladder. Buy on the published specification, not the reassurance: sensor type, what is actually measured versus inferred, battery life per charge in hours, whether the base station works without a phone, and critically whether any feature you care about sits behind a subscription. That last one is the most common source of regret in connected baby gear and it is never in the headline. Search on Amazon

3. Beats Solo 4 — Amazon. Up to 50% off, against an electronics discount band of roughly 24%, so this is running at double the category depth. On-ear headphones are an unglamorous category that got quietly good, and the honest numbers here are battery life in hours, weight in grams, whether it does lossless over USB-C, and clamping force. Weight and clamp are what decide whether you wear them past week three. Note this is the on-ear model, not over-ear — if you want isolation for a flight, that is a different product and the discount does not transfer. Search on Amazon

4. Astercook 13-Piece Kitchen Knife Set — Amazon. Sixty-six percent off, and the purest bottom-of-the-barbell buy on this list. Nearly seven in ten purchases today came in under $20, and this is the category where that behaviour is correct: a knife set is a solved problem, the marginal utility of spending five times more is real but small, and the failure mode is boredom rather than breakage. Buy on steel type and Rockwell hardness if published, and on whether the blades are full-tang. Ignore the block design entirely. Search on Amazon

5. TP-Link Wi-Fi Range Extender — Amazon. Under $20, down from about $30. This is the highest ratio of usefulness to cost in the entire guide and nobody will ever be excited about it. Check one number before buying: the wireless standard, because an extender on an older standard becomes the ceiling for everything downstream of it. Also confirm it has a wired Ethernet port — the models without one are a few dollars cheaper and dramatically less useful, since the port lets you hard-wire a console or a TV at the far end of the house. Search on Amazon

6. Hisense U7SG 55-inch 4K TV — Walmart. Around $599 after roughly $149 off. Televisions are running a 17% category band, and the reason to buy this one at Walmart specifically is structural rather than sentimental: the Walmart event runs two days longer than Amazon’s, carries no membership gate, and televisions are the category where the two retailers price-match each other most aggressively in real time. Buy on panel technology, peak brightness in nits, native refresh rate rather than the marketing number, and the count of full-bandwidth HDMI 2.1 ports. Native refresh is the specification most often obscured by an invented “motion rate” figure. Search on Walmart

7. Kendra Scott Pendant Necklace — Target. Twenty-five percent off during Circle Deal Days, and Target is the right venue for a specific reason: the basic Circle tier is free, event pricing is not gated behind a paid membership, and stacking a small jewellery buy with consumables clears the free same-day delivery threshold. Jewellery is a category where the barbell is unusually stark — either it is inexpensive enough to be disposable or it is precious-metal enough to hold value, and the middle is where the plating wears through. Check the metal specification and whether the plating thickness is stated. Search on Target

8. LoomLab Beach Graphic Tee — TikTok Shop. About $15.98, and it cleared roughly $7.88 million in quarterly revenue as the only apparel item in the platform’s entire top ten. That combination is the barbell rendered as a single product: the cheapest thing on the chart, outselling items at twenty times the price. It is worth understanding why rather than just noting it. A sub-$16 graphic tee has no research phase, no size anxiety that a return policy cannot absorb, and a decision window measured in seconds — which is exactly the shape of purchase that a short-form video feed converts best. Buy on fabric weight in GSM and composition; on a printed tee, GSM is the only number that separates one that survives ten washes from one that does not. Browse TikTok Shop

9. CaDA Licensed Mercedes-AMG Brick Set — eBay. A licensed construction set that broke into the German site’s top listings and a good illustration of where collectible value is concentrating. In remote-control and collectible toys, products above $300 now take more than 40% of top-listing share, and licensed IP drives both the traffic and the conversion. eBay is the honest venue here because sold listings give you a transaction history rather than an asking price. Filter to completed sales first, check seller feedback on sealed product specifically, and confirm the set number rather than trusting the photograph — licensed brick sets have near-identical variants at very different values. Search eBay sold listings

10. ASUS TUF Gaming Monitor — Best Buy. Up to 37% off during Tech Fest, against a computers category running only about 15%. That gap is the point: monitors get discounted like electronics, not like computers, which makes them the best-value component purchase of this particular week. Buy strictly on published panel numbers — panel type, native resolution, true refresh rate, response time measured grey-to-grey, and certified variable-refresh support rather than “compatible.” Response-time claims are the most inflated specification in the category, so weight the panel type more heavily than the millisecond figure. Search on Best Buy

Best Direct-to-Consumer & Brand Picks

Everything below was chosen on one criterion, and it is the direct consequence of the barbell: these are brands that sell at the top of their ladder and therefore have no reason to participate in a discount week at all. Several of them have never run a meaningful sale. That is not a reason to skip them today — it is a reason to look at them today, because the correct comparison for a premium direct purchase is not “is it discounted” but “what does the discounted marketplace alternative actually cost me over five years.” This week is when that arithmetic is easiest to do, because the alternative is sitting right there at 24% off.

1. Concept2 — rowing machines. Exercise equipment was up 125% today, and Concept2 is the reference point the entire category is measured against. The relevant fact about this company is that it has essentially never discounted, for decades, and its machines hold resale value better than any other piece of home fitness equipment by a wide margin. Buy on the published numbers, which are unusually complete: drag factor range, monitor generation, stroke data outputs, and the fact that parts remain available for machines twenty-plus years old. The resale floor is the real specification — a machine that sells used for a large fraction of retail has a true cost of ownership far below its sticker. Visit Concept2

2. Rogue Fitness — strength equipment. The other half of the same category and the same logic. Published steel gauge, tensile strength in PSI, knurling specification, bar whip characteristics, weight tolerance as a percentage, and warranty in years. Strength equipment is a category where the spec sheet is the entire product and where the difference between good and bad is permanent rather than stylistic. Buy on steel gauge for racks and on tensile strength plus tolerance for bars, and standardize on one hole spacing so attachments remain compatible for the next decade. Visit Rogue Fitness

3. Jackery — portable power stations. The category posted $4.53 million on a single competing SKU on TikTok in June with 247% growth, which tells you demand is real and seasonal. Buy on published capacity in watt-hours rather than amp-hours, continuous output in watts separated clearly from surge output, cell chemistry, and rated cycle life to 80% capacity. Cycle life is the number that separates a five-year purchase from a fifteen-year one and it is the one most often buried. Lithium iron phosphate cells cost more up front and last several times longer. Visit Jackery

4. Lectric eBikes — electric bicycles. Direct-only, deliberately absent from the marketplace channel, and a category that does not appear in this week’s event at all — which is exactly why it belongs on a barbell list. Buy on motor output in watts alongside torque in newton-metres, battery capacity in watt-hours rather than the range estimate, total weight including battery, and the braking system. Range figures are marketing; watt-hours are arithmetic. Check the folded dimensions against wherever it will actually live, because that is the specification people discover too late. Visit Lectric eBikes

5. Saatva — mattresses. Furniture and bedding is running about a 16% discount band this week, which is shallow, and mattresses are the classic case where the marketplace version and the direct version are not the same product. Buy on coil count and gauge, foam density in pounds per cubic foot, and the length of the trial period in nights alongside who pays return shipping. Foam density is the durability number and it is the one almost never printed on a marketplace listing. A trial that requires you to pay for removal is not really a trial. Visit Saatva

6. UPPAbaby — strollers and car seats. Strollers jumped 220% today and car seats 140%, and this brand is the reason the category behaves like a barbell: it holds price rigidly, so the sale simply does not reach it, and buyers accept that because the alternative is not a cheaper stroller but a worse one. Buy on folded dimensions in inches, weight, maximum child weight, and the specific safety standard the seat is certified to. Then check the resale market before you buy anything — this is one of very few product categories with a genuinely liquid secondhand market, which changes the real cost substantially. Visit UPPAbaby

7. Vivobarefoot — minimal footwear. Barefoot and minimal shoes have been one of the more persistent trend lines of the year, and this is a category where the numbers are unusually honest because there is so little product to hide behind. Buy on stack height in millimetres, drop in millimetres, sole thickness, and the width of the toe box at its widest point. Stack height is the whole product. If you are coming from conventional cushioned shoes, treat the transition as a multi-week process rather than a purchase — the most common bad outcome here is not a bad shoe but too fast a switch. Visit Vivobarefoot

8. Stasher — reusable silicone storage. Household goods were up 65% today and this is the durable-goods answer to a consumable line item. Buy on published silicone grade, temperature range in degrees for both freezer and oven, and whether it is genuinely one-piece moulded rather than seamed. The seam is the failure point in every competing product in this category, and a one-piece moulding is the entire reason to pay a premium here. Verify dishwasher and sous-vide ratings if you intend to use either. Visit Stasher

9. Baggu — bags and everyday carry. The cheap end of the direct barbell, generally in the mid-teens to low-twenties. Buy on ripstop denier, stated load capacity in pounds, packed dimensions, and washability. Denier is the number that tells you whether a packable bag survives a year of grocery runs. This is a category where the sensible move is to buy several of the cheap thing rather than one of the expensive thing, which is precisely the bottom-of-the-ladder logic that drove today’s sub-$20 wave. Visit Baggu

10. Salt & Stone — personal care. Personal hygiene was up 130% today, the second-steepest category jump on the entire board, and it is the most quietly competitive corner of direct-to-consumer right now. Buy on the published active ingredient and its concentration, full INCI list, and the specification for any SPF product — broad spectrum rating, water resistance in minutes, and the active filter used. Water resistance stated in minutes is a tested figure; “water resistant” on its own is not. That distinction is the single most useful thing to know when buying sun care. Visit Salt & Stone

Buying Tips for This Week

Sort your list into two piles before you open anything. Pile one is replenishment: things you have bought before, will buy again, and have no opinion about. Pile two is acquisition: things you will own for years. The barbell says to shop these completely differently, and the mistake almost everybody makes is applying one method to both. For pile one, the only variable is unit cost, and the correct behaviour is to buy in bulk and stop thinking. For pile two, the only variable is whether this is the version you still want in three years, and price is a constraint rather than a criterion.

Use the discount depth chart as a filter, not a wishlist. Electronics at 24% and apparel at 23% are real. Sporting goods at 9% is not a sale in any meaningful sense — if you need something from that category, this week gives you nothing and you should buy it whenever you actually need it. Computers at 15% is the trap, because expectations for that category run far ahead of the actual markdowns. The general rule: if the item is discounted less than its category average, the retailer is telling you it does not need to discount it, which usually means it is selling fine at full price.

Distrust anything in the middle of its own price ladder. The clearest signal in today’s data is that mid-tier products are losing share at both ends. That is also where the highest proportion of inflated reference prices live, because a mid-tier item has the most room to be marked up before being marked down. If you are looking at a $120 version of something that exists at $30 and at $400, the burden of proof on the $120 is much higher than it feels.

Today is day one of four, and the format is designed to make you act early. New deals cycle as often as every five minutes during peak periods, which sounds like scarcity and is actually the opposite — it is a system optimized to keep you refreshing. The genuinely scarce items are the doorbusters that went in the first hours; everything else is inventory that has to clear by Friday. Walmart runs through June 28, two days past Amazon, and those final two days are historically where the unsold stock gets marked properly.

Price-check across at least two of the four concurrent events. Four sales overlapping is unusual, and it means the same SKU frequently carries different prices at Amazon, Walmart, Target and Best Buy within the same hour. The categories where this matters most are televisions, small appliances and headphones, because all four retailers carry identical models and all four are watching each other. Thirty seconds of checking is worth more than an hour of deal-hunting inside a single store.

Read the specification, not the adjective — and note that this is now also how machines evaluate. AI-referred traffic converted 50.7% better today than every other channel, and the reason is mechanical: an agent parses structured data, return policy language, size-chart accuracy and review sentiment, and simply cannot see the lifestyle photography. Adopting that method yourself is free. If the important facts about a product are published numbers, you can compare it. If they are published adjectives, you cannot, and the absence is usually deliberate.

Watch for the subscription tail on connected products. This applies across baby monitors, wearables, security cameras and increasingly kitchen appliances. The hardware discount is real and the ongoing cost is invisible at checkout. Do the three-year arithmetic before you decide whether 40% off the device is actually a good number.

If you are buying from outside your own market, factor the July 1 change now. The EU’s €150 low-value exemption ends July 1 and is replaced by a flat €3 per-parcel duty, applied to both fulfilled and merchant-shipped orders arriving from outside the bloc, with the charge and its VAT added to displayed prices on pan-European inventory. On a sub-€20 item that is a material percentage. If you are in the EU and have low-value cross-border purchases queued, this week is cheaper than next week by a knowable amount.

What’s Coming Next

The immediate calendar. Prime Day runs through Friday June 26, with the deepest general markdowns historically landing on the final day rather than the first. Walmart Deals continues to June 28. Target Circle Deal Days closes with Amazon. Expect the pattern from previous four-day events: a doorbuster wave that is already gone, a mid-event lull, and a genuine clearance push in the last twenty-four to thirty-six hours on anything overstocked.

July 1 is the hard date for anyone buying or selling across borders. The EU exemption for consignments under €150 disappears and the flat €3 per-parcel duty begins. Merchant-fulfilled sellers will need designated carriers and valid IOSS registration; pan-European inventory gets the duty and VAT folded into the front-end price automatically. The knock-on effect on sub-€20 goods is severe enough that a meaningful number of low-price SKUs will stop being viable in that market, and the shelf will visibly thin out through July.

The UK now has a fixed horizon. Today’s announcement moved the removal of the £135 exemption to October 2028. That is still more than two years out, but the direction is now settled and the retail lobby is pushing for faster. The intermediate risk everyone in the trade is watching is displacement: once the EU tightens on July 1 and the UK has not yet, low-value volume that used to enter through the continent may reroute through Britain. If that happens visibly, the October 2028 date will come under pressure to move again.

Shopify Scripts stops running June 30. For shoppers this is invisible until it is not — any store still depending on Scripts for discount logic, shipping rules or payment customization will have those break at the end of the month. Expect a scattering of odd checkout behaviour on smaller independent stores in early July.

The agentic channel is going to get measurably bigger before it gets better. With the Universal Commerce Protocol switched on by default across Shopify’s merchant base, product data from a very large share of independent stores becomes readable and purchasable inside assistants without any merchant action. Combined with payment infrastructure for agents now shipping, the practical result over the next two quarters is that the set of products an assistant can actually complete a purchase on expands enormously. The gap between the merchants that are machine-readable and the roughly 46% that are not will widen into something that shows up in market share.

Back-to-school starts earlier than the calendar suggests. School supplies were already up 140% today, in June, during a sale nominally about electronics. That is the tell. The category will run hot from now through August, and the deepest genuine markdowns in it historically arrive in the second half of July rather than during a summer event.

Prime Big Deal Days in October is the next event of this size. Between now and then, the meaningful buying windows are the July 4 weekend for outdoor and grills, late July for back-to-school, and Labor Day for appliances and mattresses. Nothing between now and October will discount electronics as deeply as this week is doing.

Price Trend Outlook

Electronics will not be this cheap again until October, and possibly not then. A 24% category band with premium-tier mix up 48% is a specific combination: retailers are clearing high-end inventory rather than merely shifting volume. That happens when stock was built for a demand curve that did not materialize at the top. Once this event clears it, restocking happens at current cost, and current cost includes a tariff and logistics environment that has only moved one direction. If you have a considered electronics purchase in mind, the arithmetic favours this week over waiting.

The sub-$20 tier is going to keep expanding, and quality within it will stay volatile. Nearly seven in ten purchases today came in under $20, and platforms have noticed — the dedicated low-price storefronts are being merchandised harder every quarter. But this is precisely the tier most exposed to the de minimis changes now landing across the EU, the UK and already the US. A flat per-parcel charge is regressive by design: it barely dents a $200 item and it can erase the margin on a $6 one. Expect the very bottom of the price ladder to thin out over the next two quarters, with the survivors consolidating into fewer, larger sellers who can ship in bulk to local warehouses rather than direct from origin. Practical consequence: genuinely cheap goods will remain available, but the range of them will narrow.

Mid-tier pricing is where the discounting will get most aggressive and least meaningful. Losing share at both ends is a slow emergency, and the standard response is promotion. Over the second half of the year, expect the heaviest markdown activity, the longest sale windows and the most inflated reference prices to concentrate in exactly the $60-to-$150 band. Treat a large percentage-off number in that range as information about the seller’s inventory position rather than about the product’s value.

Baby, fitness and personal care look structurally firm. Strollers at +220%, car seats at +140%, exercise equipment at +125% and personal hygiene at +130% are not event artifacts — they are categories with either a hard safety floor or a genuine replenishment cadence underneath them. Prices in these categories tend not to fall much between events, which means the correct strategy is to buy the considered items during events and the consumables whenever the unit cost is right.

Appliances and furniture are the wait-if-you-can categories. Both are running mid-teens discount bands this week, which is shallow, and both have Labor Day as a structurally deeper event. Unless you are replacing something that has already failed, there is little cost to waiting ten weeks and a reasonable expectation of better pricing.

The longer arc: agent-legible products will hold price better than agent-invisible ones. This is speculative but the mechanism is clear enough to plan around. When a growing share of purchase decisions are finalized by a system that reads specifications and cannot see photography, products whose value is genuinely in the specification become easier to sell at full price, and products whose value is in the presentation lose their main lever. Over the next several years that should compress margins hardest on mid-market goods sold on styling, and support them on commodity goods sold on numbers. For a shopper, the useful inversion is that the brands most willing to publish hard figures today are the ones least likely to need to discount tomorrow — so buying them on sale, as this week allows, is the asymmetry worth taking.

Bottom Line

The headline from today is that Americans spent $8.3 billion online in a single day, more than any other day this year. The subplot is that they did it while spending 17% less per order than they did a year ago. Those two things are only contradictory if you assume the price ladder is a smooth curve. It is not, and today it stopped pretending to be.

What actually happened is that the middle emptied out. The bottom got bigger by volume — more orders, smaller each, seven in ten under twenty dollars, concentrated in the things people buy without deciding. The top got bigger by mix — the share of the most expensive goods sold rose nineteen percent overall and forty-eight percent in electronics, because when people did decide, they decided harder. Only 41% said the sale was even the main reason they were there, down from 52% last year, which is a polite way of saying the event has become a backdrop rather than a reason.

The practical instruction fits in a sentence: buy the cheap things in bulk and the expensive things properly, and be suspicious of everything in between, because that is where both the shrinking demand and the inflated discounts have gone to live.

The twenty picks above are built on exactly that split. On the marketplace side, five items are running at roughly double their category discount band and five are cheap enough that the decision costs less than the deliberation. On the direct side, ten brands that mostly do not discount at all — because the right comparison for a premium purchase this week is not whether it is on sale, but what the discounted alternative actually costs you by 2031.

One last thing worth carrying forward. AI-referred traffic converted 50.7% better today than every other channel, after converting 23% worse during the same event a year ago. Whatever you think about shopping assistants, the method underneath that reversal is available to you for free: read the numbers, ignore the adjectives, and if the numbers are not published, ask why not. It works because it is boring, and it will keep working for the same reason.
