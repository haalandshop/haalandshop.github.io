Best Things to Buy Right Now — July 20, 2026

Quick take: This is the trough week. Every major deal event has finished — the US ran late June, Brazil and India wrapped in the first week of July, Australia and Japan closed out around the 13th — and the back-to-school surge does not really begin until the very end of the month. That gap is where real prices live. No countdown timers, no inflated strike-through pricing, and suppliers restocked after the mid-year sourcing season, so inventory is deep and cheap. If you have been waiting to buy something without being manipulated into it, this is the week.

What’s Hot This Week

Road trip gear is at its absolute annual peak. Late July is the single heaviest driving-holiday stretch in both North America and Europe, and the accessory categories that follow it are moving hard: trunk organizers, windshield sunshades, floating phone pouches and compression storage. These are unglamorous $15–$40 items, which is exactly why they do not get discounted in deal events — they sell at the same price all summer, so buying now costs you nothing extra.

Pet cooling has quietly become one of the fastest-growing export categories of the year. Gel-filled pet mats, ice-silk cooling vests and ventilated travel crates are shipping out of Chinese supply hubs at roughly 40% monthly order growth, with the heaviest pull coming from Southeast Asia, the Middle East, South America and Japan/Korea. This is not a novelty trend — it is a heat-driven necessity purchase, and the same product wave is now landing on Western marketplaces at retail. Cooling mats that source in the $6–$12 range are selling at $24–$39, and they work.

Free-standing camping hammocks are the breakout outdoor product. The category used to be limited by a simple physical problem: you need two trees. The current viral version ships with its own folding steel frame, holds around 450 lb, and packs down to the size of a folding chair. One listing alone has moved over 33,000 units, and roughly 62% of its sales came through livestream rather than search — a good reminder that outdoor gear is now a demo category, not a spec-sheet category. The broader camping hammock market is compounding at about 6.7% a year and is on track to roughly double by the mid-2030s.

Water play is the biggest percentage mover of the season. Water toys are up well over 300% against the spring baseline and pool accessories over 200%, with lawn and garden close behind at over 200%. These numbers fall off a cliff in about three weeks, so this is a buy-now-or-wait-a-year category.

Beauty is still the strongest single vertical, but the winning product changed shape. The dominant collagen balms and repair sets are flattening out, and the growth has rotated into next-generation actives — NAD+ and EGF firming serums in particular are debuting straight into top-ten territory at around the $18 price point. Combined top-ten beauty revenue on social commerce actually fell month over month, which sounds bearish but really means the money is spreading across more products instead of concentrating in three hero SKUs. Good news for buyers: more competition, better prices.

Sunglasses are having a genuine moment. Geometric double-bridge UV400 polarized frames have been holding chart positions for weeks, blending a retro silhouette with real sun protection. At the $15–$30 tier they have become a semi-disposable summer accessory rather than an investment purchase.

Platform & Category Watch

Amazon. Post-event pricing has normalized, which counterintuitively makes this a better week than the sale itself for anything that was not a doorbuster. Outdoor, automotive accessories and pet are the three categories with the deepest current inventory. Beauty and personal care carried the deal season globally and continues to hold.

TikTok Shop. Still the fastest signal for what is about to sell everywhere else, and still overwhelmingly beauty-led across the US, UK, Malaysia, Thailand and Mexico. The interesting shift is supplements and baby/family care climbing the charts — family-care bundles in Southeast Asia are now posting some of the largest single-shop volumes on the platform. Livestream conversion is doing the heavy lifting on physical goods.

Walmart. Winning the value narrative going into back-to-school harder than anyone. Its share of parent consideration jumped more than 22 percentage points year over year to over 77%, and together with Target and Amazon it now accounts for roughly 58% of all top-retailer mentions. Dollar-store formats also cracked the top ten for the first time. If your buy is price-first, Walmart is the default this season.

Temu and AliExpress. Sourcing prices are near their annual floor after the mid-year restock cycle, which shows up at retail as unusually low pricing on sub-$30 impulse goods. Both are also under real regulatory pressure in Europe — AliExpress absorbed a €550 million penalty under the EU’s Digital Services Act, and France’s new anti-ultra-fast-fashion law explicitly names cross-border platforms as its primary targets. Practical consumer effect: expect slower European delivery promises and more local-warehouse routing, not necessarily higher headline prices yet.

Shein. Mid-transition. Net profit came in around $2.06 billion for 2025 at a 4.9% margin, then swung to a small quarterly loss in Q1 2026. The response has been a full rebuild of fulfillment — roughly 6 million square metres of warehousing now spread across Asia, North America, Europe, the Middle East and South America, with regional hubs live in Poland and Spain. Inventory turns in 36 days and about 4,700 new styles hit the site daily. The platform side of the business is now the real growth engine, climbing from $868 million in service revenue in 2023 to $4.74 billion in 2025.

Shopify and independent stores. The most underrated shift of the year. Merchants on the platform now pull an average of 31% of their gross merchandise volume from outside their home country, up from 22% in early 2024 — and for brands in the $5M–$50M band that figure is 38%. Independent sites now represent more than 35% of cross-border export volume, with average order values running 0.5x to 0.8x higher than marketplace storefronts. The 25-month legal fight between the two largest storefront platforms settled at the start of the month, clearing compliance uncertainty for over 600,000 merchants. Total global cross-border B2C volume is tracking toward $1.47 trillion this year.

The catch: the shakeout is real. Merchant-initiated market exits rose 23% year over year in Q1 2026, concentrated among brands that went international opportunistically rather than deliberately. What separates survivors is boring infrastructure — stores offering three or more locally preferred payment methods see cart abandonment roughly 31% lower than those defaulting to a single global processor. As a shopper, that is your quality signal: if a brand site shows you your own currency, your own payment method and the full landed cost including duties at checkout, it is a real operation.

Buying Tips for This Week

Buy the seasonal stuff now, not on sale. Water toys, pool accessories, beach gear and cooling products are up 200–376% against baseline and will be cleared out or gone in roughly three weeks. Waiting for a discount on a peak-season item usually means waiting for the item to disappear.

Ignore the back-to-school urgency for one more week. Surveys wildly disagree on how much families will spend this year — estimates range from a $557 per-child figure that is down about 6% in real terms, to $489 up nearly 12%, to over $900 per household. They disagree on the number but agree completely on the timing: spending peaks in late July and early August. The best selection is right now, the best pricing comes in the second and third weeks of August, and the worst of both is the last week before school starts.

Clothing over tech this season. Apparel budgets are running about 22% higher while tech is down roughly 16% — parents are simply not upgrading devices this year. That means apparel gets the promotional weight and tech does not. If you need a device, this is not the season for it; if you need clothes, competition is working in your favour.

Check the landed cost, not the sticker. With EU parcel rules tightened and platform penalties landing, the price you see on a cross-border listing increasingly is not the price you pay. Any store worth buying from will show duties and fees at checkout. If it does not, assume there is a bill coming.

Bundle same-category items into one order. Per-parcel and per-classification fees are now a real cost in Europe. Three separate shipments of similar goods can cost meaningfully more than one combined order for the same items.

Value-seeking is not the same as cheap. The most aggressive discount hunters this year — roughly a third of shoppers, using four or more cost-saving tactics — actually spend about 14% more per child than everyone else. They are not buying less; they are buying better and paying attention. That is the right posture for this week specifically.

What’s Coming Next

Now through July 27 — Last clean window for peak-summer goods at normal prices. Water play, beach, camping, road trip and pet cooling are all at maximum selection with no event-driven price distortion.

July 28 – August 8 — Back-to-school demand arrives in force. Selection is at its best but so is competition; mass merchants take roughly 80% of planned spending, so Walmart, Target and Amazon set the price floor. Apparel gets the promotional weight, tech does not.

Mid-August — The crossover. Summer categories go to clearance while school categories are still fully priced. This is the best week of the year to buy next summer’s pool, beach and cooling gear at 50–70% off, and the worst week to buy a backpack.

Late August — UK and EU back-to-school peaks land here, a few weeks behind North America. If you are buying cross-border into Europe, order by the second week of August to clear customs in time.

September onward — Cooling gear dies, and the demand shifts to air purifiers, heated blankets, hot-drink equipment and indoor comfort. Pet cooling mats hit clearance; pet warming and indoor enrichment products take over.

Q4 setup — Watch the independent-store channel. With cross-border volume heading toward $1.47 trillion and brand sites carrying meaningfully higher order values, the better holiday offers are increasingly landing on brand storefronts before marketplaces, not after.

Bottom Line

The absence of a sale is not the absence of an opportunity. This week has no manufactured urgency, deep post-restock inventory, and genuine peak-season selection across outdoor, automotive, pet and beach categories. Buy the things that are seasonal and will vanish — hammocks, cooling mats, water gear, sunshades — and deliberately wait on the things that are about to get promotional weight thrown at them, which is essentially anything with a school logo on it. In three weeks the calculus flips completely.
