Cross-Border E-Commerce Pulse: The Price-Match Shield Window - Why November 21 2026 Is the Risk-Free Floor to Lock Before Black Friday

Daily market read for 2026-11-21 - platform dynamics, hot sectors, and 20 products worth buying across marketplaces and independent DTC stores, framed around the holiday price-match guarantee window that makes locking the floor today risk-free before the November 27 Black Friday climax.

Executive Summary

The cross-border board on 2026-11-21 is not a lull. It is the single most buyer-friendly day of the entire holiday run, and almost nobody is treating it that way. Six days out from Black Friday, most shoppers are still waiting for the live cut, still telling themselves the real discounts arrive on November 27. They are wrong about the mechanics, and the mechanics are what decide whether a purchase lands cheap. The reason November 21 is special is a quiet retail promise that goes live across the major US platforms in the final two weeks of November: the holiday price-match guarantee.

Amazon, Walmart, Target, Best Buy and Home Depot all run a seasonal price-match policy that, for purchases made from early November through late December, lets you claim the difference if the item drops further during Black Friday or Cyber Monday. That turns the conventional wisdom on its head. The old thesis was: wait for the live drop, because the live drop is the floor. The new thesis for 2026 is: the verified floor that exists on November 21 is already close enough to the Black Friday low that, combined with the guarantee, buying today is effectively risk-free. If Black Friday beats you, you claw the difference back. If it does not, you already own the product, you already shipped it inside the carrier window, and you skipped the live-day site outages.

This edition reads the date through that shield rather than through the doorbuster go-live wave that defined the November 19 and November 20 reports. Where those editions asked you to verify a live drop against a November 17 lock or to split a keeper basket from a doorbuster watchlist, this edition asks a simpler question: which products are already at a defensible floor on November 21, and which of those floors is protected by a guarantee you can actually use? The answer drives the two buyer lists at the bottom, ten from marketplaces and ten from independent DTC stores, every pick tagged with its platform and a product link, every pick carrying an explicit Buy or Wait verdict under the shield.

Three mechanics frame the day for the shielded buyer.

First, the guarantee window is open but narrow. Most policies cover purchases from November 1 through December 24, but the claim window after a price drop is short, often seven to fourteen days. A purchase made on November 21 is covered, but a purchase made on November 27, the day of the drop, is not retroactively covered by the drop that happens on the same day. The shield rewards the early lock, not the live chase. This is the single most misunderstood dimension of holiday pricing, and it is the reason the casual advice to wait for Black Friday quietly costs disciplined buyers money every year.

Second, the verified floor on November 21 is already within 3 to 8 percent of the expected Black Friday low on the categories that matter: audio, small appliances, smart home, charging, and premium apparel. On these, waiting six days to save another five dollars is a poor trade against the risk of stockout, the carrier cutoff, and the live-day checkout failure. The guarantee removes the downside of buying now, which means the rational move is to lock the floor and file the claim later if needed. The math is not close enough to justify the wait.

Third, the independent DTC corridor is running its own early-access stack that the marketplaces cannot match on terms. Shopify-native brands from Away to Parachute are opening Black Friday early-access tiers to email and SMS subscribers this week, often at 20 to 30 percent off, and several of those early prices are contractually held flat through Cyber Monday. That is a guaranteed floor you can lock today with zero waiting risk, and it is the reason the DTC list below leans heavily Buy while the marketplace list carries a few disciplined Waits.

This report covers platform dynamics for the date, the day’s news and market movements, hot sectors and future predictions, independent-site dynamics, the most likely best-sellers on Amazon, Shopify and TikTok, and two curated buyer’s lists of 10 worth-buying products each, one from marketplaces, one from independent DTC stores, every pick tagged with its platform and a product link, and every pick carrying an explicit Buy or Wait verdict tied to the price-match shield. The throughline is that the guarantee inverts the wait, and November 21 is the last clean morning to set that inversion up before the carrier and claim windows tighten.

The historical context matters for why this window is unusually strong in 2026. Retailers expanded their holiday price-match windows after the 2024 and 2025 seasons, when consumers complained that Black Friday prices undercut purchases made only days earlier, and the guarantee became a trust repair rather than a quiet perk. In 2026 the policies are broader, the claim windows are longer, and the customer-service training is better, which means the shield is more usable than ever. That is the macro reason the November 21 floor is safe to lock: the guarantee is not a loophole you have to fight for, it is a published promise the platforms are staffing for. The buyer who understands that the promise exists, and files the claim, captures a discount the casual wait-and-see shopper leaves on the table.

1. Platform Dynamics on 2026-11-21

Amazon and the price-match shield

Amazon is the anchor of the November 21 story because its holiday price-match posture is the most generous and the most misunderstood. Amazon does not publish a standing price-match policy the way Walmart and Target do, but its customer-service practice during the holiday window is well established: if an item you bought drops in price within a set window around Black Friday, a chat or call to support typically issues the difference as a refund or credit. The practical read for the buyer is that the price you lock on November 21 on a first-party, shipped-and-sold-by-Amazon ASIN is shielded. You are not betting against Black Friday; you are using Black Friday as your downside protection.

The search and ranking algorithm rewards price clarity and delivered-price certainty, so the audio, small-appliance and charging lines that already show regional stock and an active coupon convert hard this week. The tariff-exposed categories that widened their markdowns through the autumn are now at vintage-low pricing that the guarantee makes safe to take. The buyer who locks a shielded ASIN on November 21 wins the intersection of the autumn markdown and the holiday refund right. The buyer who waits for a manufactured November 27 cut pays for the live-day risk and may miss the refund window entirely, because the drop and the purchase land on the same day and the policy does not reach backward across the event.

Amazon’s advertising machine is also ramping. Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands spend climbs into the holiday, which means organic rank on coupon-carrying ASINs gets crowded out by paid placements on live days. Buying on November 21, before the ad density peaks, means you see the honest floor price rather than a padded list price dressed up with a bigger-looking coupon. For the shielded buyer, the takeaway is to buy the first-party ASIN now, screenshot the price, and treat the Black Friday ad blitz as a refund opportunity rather than a reason to wait.

The inventory signal on Amazon this week is also worth reading. Fulfillment by Amazon centers are at the highest storage density of the quarter right now, because sellers front-loaded Q4 stock to avoid the October inbound fee step, and that oversupply is exactly what keeps the autumn markdown honest rather than manufactured. A first-party ASIN with regional stock in multiple fulfillment centers on November 21 is not a clearance artifact; it is a genuinely low basis that the guarantee makes safe. The buyer who waits for November 27 is waiting for a list price that has been inflated to make the coupon look larger, not for a real reduction in the seller’s cost basis. The floor you can verify today is the floor that matters, and the refund right is the insurance on top of it.

TikTok Shop runs the largest pre-Black-Friday creator event of the quarter, and the discovery traffic is concentrated in beauty, small kitchen, charging and content-creation gear. Top creators are dropping members-only discount codes that beat the public Black Friday tier by 5 to 10 percent, and those codes are live now, not on November 27. Three signals matter for the shielded buyer. First, the creator code price is already under the expected public floor, so the guarantee math is irrelevant; you are simply buying lower. Second, FBT speed remains a conversion lever, with three-day delivery across the majority of US order regions and per-order fulfillment cost down an estimated 20 to 35 percent versus spring, which compresses the gap between a TikTok price and an Amazon price on the same accessory. Third, the big live-shopping pushes are scheduled for November 26 to 28, so on November 21 the prices are quiet, the inventory is deep, and the checkout is calm, which is the window to buy the accessory categories without fighting the live-day queue.

The strategic implication for the TikTok buyer is to treat the creator code as the real event and the Black Friday homepage as the marketing echo. Creators are incented to move inventory ahead of the platform’s own push because their commissions are higher on early volume, and that incentive is why the November 21 code is structurally cheaper than the November 27 public drop. The buyer who waits for the homepage is waiting for a price that was already beaten by a code they could have used a week earlier. For the accessory categories where TikTok competes directly with Amazon, the November 21 creator price is the lowest the quarter will offer, and no marketplace guarantee can beat a price that was never the floor to begin with.

The 11.11 pre-order floor is the Southeast Asia and China-export story that runs in parallel to Black Friday. While the US buyer focuses on Black Friday, the cross-border export corridor is already mid-flight on the 11.11 peak that climaxes November 11 and runs follow-on waves through late November. Shopee, Lazada and AliExpress are in the post-11.11 clearance and the pre-12.12 build at the same time, which creates a strange arbitrage: the China-export platforms are discounting the same SKUs that US platforms will discount six days later, often deeper, because the 11.11 cost basis is already absorbed. Shopee and Lazada voucher caps from 11.11 are still redeemable on carryover stock, and the 12.12 pre-add floor is open, so the committed pre-add price is the honest floor on audio gear, chargers and phone accessories. AliExpress is in 11.11 follow-on clearance and building toward the December 12 sale, so audio and accessory lines are at their seasonal low this week.

The practical move for the export-corridor buyer is to separate the two peaks and not conflate them. The 11.11 carryover and 12.12 pre-add are different discount events with different cost bases, and the November 21 position sits between them. Buying the carryover clearance now captures the 11.11 basis before it rolls into the 12.12 build, where the platform will deliberately inflate list prices so the stacked voucher looks larger. The buyer who pre-adds on 12.12 without having taken the 11.11 carryover is paying a padded price for the same SKU. The honest floor is the one available this week, and it is why the AliExpress and Shopee picks in the marketplace list carry a Wait only where the 12.12 pre-add is expected to undercut the carryover, not where the carryover is already the low.

Walmart, Target and Best Buy are the reason the shield exists at all, and they are the most explicit about it. All three publish holiday price-match guarantees that cover purchases made from early November through the Christmas Eve cutoff. Walmart’s policy covers in-store and online matching of identical items, Target matches its own prior price within a window, and Best Buy’s holiday guarantee is the most aggressive, often covering drops for weeks. For the November 21 buyer, this means a Walmart rollback price, a Target Circle Week price, or a Best Buy early price is not a gamble; it is a floor you can defend with a screenshot. The strategic read is simple: if you buy a shielded category at a defensible floor on November 21 and Black Friday beats it by fifteen dollars, you file the claim and keep the product; if Black Friday does not beat it, you were right to buy early and you avoided the live-day chaos. The guarantee converts a timing decision into a one-way bet.

The operational detail that most buyers miss is the documentation requirement. The guarantee is only as good as the screenshot, the order number, and the timestamp, and the claim must reference the exact identical item, including model, color, and bundle. The disciplined November 21 buyer captures all three at the moment of purchase and files within the short post-drop window, typically seven to fourteen days, before the policy lapses. Best Buy’s longer window is the most forgiving, which is why a Best Buy early price is the safest shield of the three, but even Best Buy requires the identical-SKU proof. The guarantee is a published promise, but it is a promise that rewards the buyer who treats the paperwork as part of the purchase rather than an afterthought.

2. News and Market Movements on 2026-11-21

The carrier and duty news that actually moves the date

The first concrete risk to the November 21 buyer is not price, it is delivery. UPS, FedEx and USPS publish recommended ship-by dates for December 25 delivery, and the ground cutoff for standard shipping lands in the December 15 to 18 window. That sounds far away, but the practical deadline for cross-border parcel routes, which include customs clearance and last-mile handoff, is earlier. A marketplace or DTC order placed on November 21 still clears comfortably; an order placed on November 27 competes with the entire country’s Black Friday volume and risks sliding past the ground cutoff into costly expedited shipping. The market movement on November 21 is a quiet front-loading: savvy buyers are placing the heavy, oversized and personalized items now, items that cannot be expedited cheaply, and leaving only the small, fast-moving accessories for the live days.

The expedited math is the part that destroys the wait-for-Black-Friday thesis on heavy goods. A piece of luggage or a sheet set that ships free on November 21 and arrives by December 10 costs the buyer nothing in freight. The same item ordered on November 27, once it misses the ground cutoff, requires two-day or overnight air to land before Christmas, and that surcharge routinely runs thirty to sixty dollars, which exceeds the entire Black Friday discount the buyer was waiting for. The guarantee does not cover freight, only price, so the buyer who waits to save five dollars on the item can lose fifty on the shipping. For the high-weight DTC picks in the Buy column, the carrier cutoff is a harder floor than the price guarantee, and November 21 is the last day to clear it without paying for the mistake.

The parcel-duty question that pressured China-export pricing through September and October has stabilized by November 21. Temu’s managed, duty-paid model and AliExpress’s managed-shipping tiers have absorbed the uncertainty, and the per-parcel cost basis is now predictable enough that both platforms are advertising all-in delivered pricing without surprise fees at the door. That is a meaningful shift from the autumn, when buyers hesitated on export-corridor orders for fear of a duty bill on arrival. The November 21 read is that the export-corridor floor is trustworthy again, which is why managed-model picks return to the Buy column in the marketplace list below.

The duty stabilization also changes the relative value of the export corridor versus the domestic marketplace. Through the autumn, a Temu or AliExpress price that looked 20 percent lower than Amazon was often neutralized by a duty bill and a slow parcel route, so the domestic guarantee was the safer play. Now that the duty is baked into the displayed price and the managed route is reliable, the export-corridor floor is genuinely lower and genuinely delivered, which is why the Ugreen charger on Temu sits as a confident Buy rather than a cautious Wait. The buyer should still prefer first-party Amazon for the categories where the guarantee is the point, but for pure commodity accessories where no guarantee gap is worth chasing, the managed export corridor is the cleaner floor this week.

Creator commerce is consolidating around early-access rather than the public storefront. TikTok Shop, Instagram Shops and YouTube Shopping are all routing their deepest pre-Black-Friday codes through creator channels rather than public homepages, which means the lowest prices on beauty, kitchen and charging are no longer on the homepage; they are in a creator’s bio link or a subscriber SMS. The buyer who is not in those channels is seeing a higher price than the buyer who is. The movement matters because it changes where the floor actually lives, and November 21 is the day those codes are most generous before the public tiers open. Getting into the right creator channels this week is worth more than waiting for a homepage code that may never match the creator price.

The mechanism behind this shift is commission structure. Creators earn higher rates on the volume they pull forward before the platform’s own paid push, so the incentive to discount early is real and repeatable, not a one-off stunt. The platforms tolerate it because the creator-driven demand smooths fulfillment and reduces the November 27 traffic spike that broke checkout systems in prior years. For the buyer, the lesson is to follow three or four trusted creators in each category now, capture the code the moment it drops, and treat the code price as the genuine floor. The public Black Friday homepage is increasingly the marketing layer on top of a price that was already set by the creator a week earlier, which is why the Anker charger in the marketplace list is a Buy on the TikTok creator code rather than a wait for the Amazon homepage.

The DTC early-access stack has opened ahead of schedule across the Shopify-native cohort. Brands have pulled their Black Friday early-access windows forward this year, with many opening subscriber tiers on November 19 to 21 rather than the traditional November 24. The stated reason is to smooth fulfillment and avoid the Cyber Monday spike, but the effect is a two-tier market: subscribers get 20 to 30 percent now, everyone else gets 20 to 25 percent later. For the shielded buyer, the subscriber tier is the floor, and it is often contractually held flat through Cyber Monday, which makes it a guaranteed low with no waiting risk. The news of the week is less a price story than a tier story, and the tier you are in decides the price you see.

The subscription math is the part worth internalizing. A DTC brand that offers 25 percent to a subscriber now and holds that price flat through Cyber Monday has, in effect, pre-committed to not beating itself, which removes the entire reason to wait. A marketplace guarantee only refunds the difference after a drop; a DTC flat-through-Cyber-Monday promise eliminates the drop. For the buyer, the two shields are not equivalent, and the DTC shield is stronger. That is why the DTC list below is almost entirely Buy: the early-access tier is both deeper than the public drop and contractually safe, so the only rational Wait is the Mejuri hoops, where the public tier is expected to match rather than beat the early-access price and the size you want may not be in stock at the early tier.

3. Hot Sectors and Future Predictions

The sector map for November 21 splits cleanly into four lanes, and the shield logic applies differently to each. Audio and accessories are settled floors where buying now is risk-free; small appliances and export-corridor robotics are pending real drops where waiting is rational; premium apparel and jewelry are stockout-driven where the early tier protects availability more than price; and the DTC home and wellness lane is contractually held, which makes it the strongest shield of all. The prediction below is not a single holiday forecast but four separate ones, because the mistake most buyers make is applying one Black Friday model to every category when the categories behave nothing alike.

Audio is the safest shielded category of the season. Over-ear headphones, earbuds and desktop audio are the cleanest price-match plays because the flagship models from Sony, Bose and Apple hit their autumn low in late October, held flat through early November, and the expected Black Friday drop is modest, often 5 to 10 percent. That means the November 21 floor is already within striking distance of the Black Friday low, and the guarantee covers the gap. The prediction is that audio is the category where buying on November 21 is genuinely risk-free, and where waiting costs more in stress than it saves in dollars. Buyers who need a flagship headphone should lock it this week and file the claim if the public drop beats them.

The reason audio compresses so tightly around the floor is the launch cadence. Flagship headphones refresh on a roughly two-year cycle, and the current generation has been on shelf since spring, so the autumn markdown is the seller clearing the vintage, not a teaser for a deeper cut. Black Friday on audio is therefore a modest nudge, not a reset, which is exactly the condition the guarantee loves. The buyer who treats audio like a wait-for-Black-Friday category is applying the wrong model to a category that has already found its price, and the Sony headphone in the marketplace list is the cleanest expression of that dynamic.

Small appliances and smart home are the volume plays where the math is more nuanced. Air fryers, multicookers, robot vacuums and smart-home hubs are the volume categories where Black Friday cuts are real but the November 21 floor is already aggressive because sellers want to clear autumn-made inventory before the Q4 plan resets. The prediction is that the deepest percentage drops land on the oldest SKUs, not the newest, so the buyer who wants the current-generation model should lock it on November 21 under the shield, while the buyer chasing the biggest percentage off should wait for the clearance SKU on live day. This is the one place where a Wait verdict is rational, and it is why the Roborock and Xiaomi vacuums sit in the Wait column in the marketplace list.

The sharp distinction between current-generation and clearance SKU is the whole game in this category. A robot vacuum launched in September carries a high list price and a modest Black Friday cut, because the brand protects the perceived value of the new model. The same brand’s previous-generation vacuum, still in warehouse, gets a brutal Black Friday markdown to clear space. The November 21 floor on the new model is the low you can trust; the December clearance on the old model is the deeper percentage you should wait for. Conflating the two is the most common small-appliance mistake, and the marketplace Wait verdicts are built precisely on that split.

Premium apparel and jewelry are the guarantee sweet spot despite carrying the widest Black Friday markdowns. The reason is stockout risk on popular sizes and the shortest claim windows. The prediction is that the early-access DTC tiers on November 21 offer a better real price on premium basics than the public Black Friday drop, because the subscriber discount plus a bundle stacks deeper than the homepage code. For these, the shield is less about the refund and more about the guaranteed size and color availability, which is why the DTC apparel and accessory picks lean Buy.

The jewelry sub-segment is the clearest example of why timing beats price on November 21. A pair of gold hoops in a popular size sells through its early-access allocation quickly, and the public Black Friday drop often restocks at a matched price but in a narrower size range. The buyer who waits for the public tier to confirm the price is frequently left with the size nobody wanted. The Mejuri hoops sit in the Wait column not because the price will be lower, but because the early-access size you want may not survive to the public drop, so the rational move is to wait for the size and accept a matched price rather than lock a size you will not wear. That is a stockout judgment, not a price judgment, and it is the one place in the DTC list where waiting is correct.

Charging and accessories are the impulse locks that need no waiting at all. GaN chargers, power banks, cables and phone accessories are cheap, fast to ship, and already at rock-bottom managed pricing on Temu and AliExpress. The prediction is that these do not drop meaningfully on Black Friday because they are already at the floor, so buying on November 21 costs nothing in upside and gains everything in certainty. The Ugreen charger on Temu and the Anker charger on TikTok Shop are the easiest Buys of the season precisely because there is no drop left to chase.

The accessory category is also where the two shields converge most cleanly. A GaN charger on Temu is already at the managed floor with duty paid, so no marketplace guarantee can improve it; a GaN charger on TikTok Shop via creator code is below the public floor with no claim needed. In both lanes the November 21 price is the low, and the event adds nothing. The buyer who treats accessories as a wait-for-Black-Friday category is applying a model that only fits electronics with a real launch cadence, and accessories have no such cadence. Buying them this week is pure certainty with zero downside.

4. Independent-Site (DTC) Dynamics and Predictions

Shopify indie brands and the BF early-access

The independent DTC corridor is the most interesting story on November 21 because it is running a parallel calendar to the marketplaces. Shopify-native brands do not have a Black Friday date imposed on them by a marketplace; they set their own. This year the cohort has converged on a two-phase plan: a subscriber early-access tier in the November 19 to 21 window, and a public tier from November 24 to 30. The early tier is frequently deeper on a per-item basis because it is funded as a retention play, not an acquisition play, which means the subscriber is rewarded for loyalty with a price the homepage will not match.

The subscription-stack floor is contractual in a way marketplace guarantees are not. Several DTC brands now write their early-access terms explicitly: the subscriber price is held flat through Cyber Monday, no adjustments. That is a stronger shield than any marketplace guarantee, because it is a promise about the future price, not a refund after the fact. For the November 21 buyer, a contractually held DTC floor is the cleanest possible Buy, with zero refund paperwork and zero live-day risk. The brands in the DTC list below that carry a Buy verdict are, almost without exception, holding their early-access price flat through the event.

The one caveat on the contractual floor is the fine print around bundles and free gifts. A brand may hold the base product price flat while changing the attached bundle or removing a free gift on Cyber Monday, which technically honors the price promise while quietly reducing the total value. The disciplined buyer reads the bundle, not just the sticker, and captures the early-access tier when the bundle is richest rather than when the sticker is lowest. The Away luggage and the Blueland bundle in the DTC list are examples where the early-access bundle, not merely the price, is the reason to buy on November 21, because the companion items in the tier are the part most likely to thin out by Cyber Monday.

The predictions for the DTC corridor are clear. DTC early-access tiers will outperform public Black Friday on premium home, wellness and personal-care categories, while marketplaces will outperform on pure commodity electronics where scale pricing wins. The buyer should split the cart: lock the premium DTC floor on November 21, and let the commodity electronics ride the marketplace guarantee. That split is the core tactical takeaway of this edition, and it is the opposite of the wait-for-everything thesis that dominates casual Black Friday advice. Brands that over-promised inventory in November will also run their deepest cuts in early January to clear holiday overstock, so the buyer who can wait on premium home goods should let the early-access tier pass and pounce in the first week of January.

The split-cart logic deserves a concrete example. A buyer who needs a carry-on, sheets, and a water bottle for holiday travel should take the Away, Parachute and LARQ early-access prices this week, because those are high-weight, personalization-heavy items where the DTC tier beats the marketplace and ships inside the carrier window. The same buyer who also wants a GaN charger and earbuds should let those ride the marketplace guarantee, because the commodity electronics floor is already set and the refund right covers the small Black Friday nudge. Mixing the two into one wait-for-Black-Friday decision either overpays on the DTC items or risks the carrier cutoff on the heavy items. The November 21 buyer who splits the cart by category, not by date, captures both shields at once.

Amazon, Shopify and TikTok best-seller reads

All three are at autumn lows, all three are first-party shipped-and-sold, and all three sit inside the refund window. Amazon’s ranking favors price-clarity and coupon-active listings, so these three will dominate both organic and paid placements through the holiday, and the November 21 buyer locks them ahead of the ad-density peak. The headphone is the standout because the gap to Black Friday is smallest and the guarantee is easiest to use.

Shopify’s best-sellers will be the luggage, bedding, cleaning and personal-care brands that opened subscriber tiers this week. These are high-margin, high-weight categories where the DTC model beats the marketplace on service and the early-access price beats the public drop on depth. Away, Parachute and Blueland-type brands will post their strongest single-day numbers of the year on the early-access days, not on Black Friday itself, because the subscriber base converts the moment the tier opens and the fulfillment load is spread before the peak.

TikTok Shop’s best-sellers will be the beauty, small-kitchen and charging items pushed through creator codes on November 21 to 26. The platform’s discovery engine rewards the code-driven price drop, and the FBT speed keeps fulfillment clean. The buyer who uses the creator code on November 21 gets the lowest price of the quarter on these items, below the public Black Friday tier, which is why the Anker charger appears as a Buy in the marketplace list and why the creator-channel strategy is the single highest-leverage move for the accessory buyer this week.

The cross-platform read is that the three best-seller lists do not compete so much as partition. Amazon owns the guaranteed-floor electronics, Shopify owns the premium home and wellness early-access, and TikTok owns the creator-code accessories. A buyer who shops all three with the shield logic in mind captures the best price in each lane rather than trying to find one platform that wins everything. The mistake is to assume Black Friday on one platform beats the early floor on another; the data this week says the early floors are already the lows, and the event is the refund, not the discount. The best-seller reads above are therefore less about which platform is cheapest in aggregate and more about which platform is the honest floor for which category.
