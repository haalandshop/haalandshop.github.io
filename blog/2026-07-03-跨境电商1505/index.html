Best Things to Buy Right Now — Week of July 3, 2026 Shopping Guide

orcalva • Wed Nov 09 2022 •
#shopping guide#best buys#freight rates#landed cost#restock gap#marketplace picks#dtc brands#cooling appliances#back to school#summer sales

Quick take: July 1 was a handover day. Every major container line put a rate increase into effect on the same date, and Amazon stopped doing prep and labelling work for sellers across Europe and the UK — having already stopped in the US in January. Both changes push cost and effort back onto the people who sell you things. Neither one has reached a price tag yet, because the goods sitting in warehouses today were shipped in April and May at April and May prices. That inventory is the cheapest it will be this year. The replacement container is being quoted right now at thirty to fifty per cent above what the seller budgeted. So: if something is in stock, ships domestically, and you were going to buy it in the next few months anyway, buy it in the next few weeks instead.

The two things that happened on July 1

They look unrelated. They are not. Both are the same move — a large intermediary deciding it will no longer absorb a cost, and handing it down the chain until it reaches you.

One: the ocean got repriced, all at once. Container lines coordinate rate increases by announcing them for the same effective date, and July 1 was the date they all chose. COSCO implemented a general rate increase on everything moving from the Far East, the Indian Subcontinent, the Middle East and Oceania into the US and Canada — from about $2,400 on a twenty-foot box up to roughly $3,798 on a forty-five-foot one, covering West Coast, East Coast, Gulf and intermodal routings alike. MSC put a general rate increase in for the first half of the month. ONE, Hapag-Lloyd and HMM all moved on the same day. CMA CGM added a peak season surcharge of $1,400 per TEU on Far East to Mediterranean and North Africa lanes, plus separate East Africa surcharges of $500 to $1,100 depending on the Chinese port of origin, and layered a hazardous cargo surcharge and an environmental compliance surcharge on top of its own July 1 increase.

The indices confirm it is real and not just an announcement. The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index reached 3,239 on June 26, up 117 points in a single reading, after having already jumped 9.5 per cent in one week earlier in the month. The benchmark world container index climbed to $4,530 on July 2. Shanghai to Los Angeles was running around $5,750 per forty-foot container by late June; Shanghai to New York, about $7,149. East Coast transpacific rates are now above last summer’s peak. Space is described as extremely tight out of both China and Vietnam, Rotterdam and Antwerp are congested enough that vessels are bunching, and Hamburg has inland rail delays on top of that.

The number that matters to a shopper is this one: any import cost model built before March 2026 is understating actual landed container cost by thirty to fifty per cent. Sellers who priced their autumn range in the spring have a hole in their margin, and they will close it the only two ways available — raise the price, or reduce the quality of what arrives in the box.

Two: Amazon stopped doing the fiddly work. From July 1, Amazon no longer offers prep or label services for FBA shipments in its European and UK stores. That covers direct FBA shipments, Amazon Warehousing and Distribution, Amazon SEND, Amazon Global Logistics, the Supply Chain Portal, and Multi-Channel Fulfilment. The specific services that disappeared are poly-bagging, bubble-wrap protection, FNSKU barcode application, and kitting or bundling. The US version of this change already happened on January 1.

There is no grace period and no rework step at the receiving dock. A unit that turns up without its barcode label, or without the poly-bag its category requires, gets held, returned or disposed of. If it is damaged because the prep was missing, there is no reimbursement. Repeated failures affect a seller’s ability to book inbound capacity at the fulfilment centres they want.

Put the two together and you get the shape of the next quarter. The seller’s freight bill went up sharply, and simultaneously they inherited a manual labour step they used to be able to outsource to Amazon for a per-unit fee. Small sellers will absorb neither well. Some will raise prices. Some will ship smaller quantities more often, which costs more per unit and increases the chance of running out. Some will simply not restock a slow line at all.

The restock gap

Here is the practical framework for the next few months, and it is the only thing you actually need to remember from this article.

Consumer goods on a shelf in your country today crossed the ocean roughly eight to twelve weeks ago. They were bought at spring freight rates by a seller who had Amazon’s prep safety net. That stock is priced against a cost base that no longer exists.

When it sells out, the replacement arrives at July’s freight rates, prepped by a third party the seller had to go and find and pay, at a per-unit cost they had not planned for. For most categories that replacement lands in September and October — right into the run-up to the fourth quarter, which is exactly when nobody discounts.

The gap between those two moments is the buying window. It is open now and it closes when the current inventory runs down.

Some categories are far more exposed than others, and this is where the framework becomes useful rather than just interesting:

  • Heavily exposed — anything bulky, heavy, or low-value-per-cubic-metre shipped from Asia. Fans, air conditioners, furniture, garden equipment, luggage, bulk homeware, most small appliances. Freight is a large share of the landed cost, so a fifty per cent freight increase moves the retail price meaningfully.
  • Moderately exposed — consumer electronics, tools, footwear. Freight is a smaller share, but volumes are high and margins are thin, so increases still pass through.
  • Barely exposed — anything made in your own country, anything already in the second-hand or resale market, anything made to order locally, and most beauty and personal care produced regionally. These are the categories where waiting costs you nothing.

The mistake people will make this autumn is assuming a price rise means the product got better. It did not. It got shipped.

What is actually selling

The demand side is doing something simpler and more predictable than the supply side.

Cooling is still the whole story in Europe. Air conditioner penetration across European homes is still under twenty per cent, which means every hot week converts directly into appliance demand rather than being absorbed by existing installations. The global outdoor cooling equipment market is running at $6.24 billion this year, and cooling-feel apparel — the fabrics marketed as cold-touch — at $3.95 billion, growing around 7.8 per cent. Sun protection has quietly stopped being seasonal: the global outdoor sun-protection category has passed $19.2 billion, with sun-protective clothing, vehicle window shades and sports sun protection all compounding at about 7.2 per cent a year. In the UK, ice maker sales are running roughly ten times last year. A single handheld fan model shifted 34,100 units in the German market in a little over two months.

On TikTok Shop, the interesting move is auctions. Live auction GMV in the US is running about four times that of a standard livestream, and through the first four months of the year auction GMV grew at roughly fourteen times the rate of short-form video commerce. The platform has dropped the invitation-only requirement, and the categories it is steering into the format are the ones where price discovery is genuinely uncertain — collectibles and designer toys, jewellery, and second-hand luxury. That is a rational match. An auction is the right mechanism for a scarce item and the wrong one for a commodity, and if you see a mass-produced item being auctioned, the scarcity is manufactured.

For the last full week of June, the platform’s own bestsellers tell you where attention is: in the US, medicube’s PDRN collagen eye patches took the top GMV spot, with anti-ageing, body deodorising and AHA products holding. In the UK, the heat pushed LED ceiling fan lights, desk fans and cooling misting devices up from nowhere. In Mexico, women’s activewear — running shorts and yoga sets — plus a mid-range AI phone and multivitamins.

Payout speed is now a proxy for seller quality. From July, TikTok Shop in the US ties settlement timing to the Shop Performance Score: a score of 3.5 or above gets paid in five days, 4.0 or above in one. Across Southeast Asia and the UK, the old violation-points system has been replaced by an Account Health Rating that ranks negative review rates against the category median rather than a fixed threshold. Southeast Asian cross-border settlement moved from three tiers to four in June, with payout speed tied to performance. All of this quietly favours sellers who already fulfil well — which is the same group whose stock is already in your country.

Amazon is loosening one gate and tightening another. From July it begins phasing out some of the hard performance thresholds on Featured Offer — the Buy Box — eligibility, with global rollout planned by year end. Order defect rate, late shipment rate and valid tracking rate will still count towards account health but will stop being standalone eligibility gates. More sellers will therefore appear in the Buy Box. Combined with the end of prep services, that means the default offer on a listing may now come from a seller with weaker operational metrics than before. Check who is actually shipping the thing.

Shopify’s Spring 2026 Edition, with its 150-plus updates, was mostly about making catalogues legible to AI assistants via the Universal Commerce Protocol and a Catalog API. The long-run implication for independent stores is that discovery migrates from search engines to AI recommendation, and structured product data starts to matter more than page copy. For a shopper in July 2026 the effect is small but real: brands that have done this work will start showing up in assistant-mediated shopping, and they tend to be the better-run ones.

And the map of Southeast Asia moved. TikTok Shop’s Thailand revenue hit 54.4 billion baht last year, roughly quadruple the prior year, overtaking Lazada’s 30.1 billion to take second place. Shopee still leads at 88.3 billion but its share fell about six percentage points. TikTok Shop’s Vietnamese share is now around 24 per cent and Thailand close to 20. If you buy from the region, the platform with the best inventory is no longer the one it was eighteen months ago.

How to buy this week

Treat “in stock and ships domestically” as a price, not a convenience. It is the same product at the same number today. In September it is a different number, or it is unavailable. This is the whole thesis and everything below is detail.

Work out which band your purchase falls into before you decide to wait. Bulky and imported: buy now. Small, high-value and imported: buy now if you were buying within six months anyway. Made locally, made to order, or second-hand: waiting costs you nothing, so wait for a genuine sale.

Fix the cause before you buy the cure. This has been true all summer and the freight story makes it more true, because the fix is cheaper and more local than the appliance. External blinds, reflective window film, thermal curtains, draught sealing and a decent ceiling fan are mostly domestic goods sold by domestic retailers. A portable air conditioner is an imported appliance whose price is about to move. Buying the blind is both the better thermodynamics and the better economics.

Check who is behind the Buy Box now, not who was behind it in May. With performance thresholds being phased out of Featured Offer eligibility, the default seller on a listing can change without the listing changing. Open the seller name. If it is a marketplace third party rather than the brand or the retailer, look at whether the item ships from inside your country. That one field predicts both the delivery date and whether the order survives.

Expect more sudden out-of-stocks than usual, and act on availability. The end of prep services means units can be refused at the receiving dock over a missing barcode or a missing poly-bag. That failure mode produces an abrupt disappearance rather than a gradual sell-through. If you find the exact configuration you want in stock — the right size, the right colour, the right variant — that is the buying signal. Coming back next week is a worse bet than it was a month ago.

Be more sceptical of bundles from small sellers. Kitting and bundling were things Amazon used to do at the fulfilment centre. Now the seller has to assemble the multipack themselves before it ships. Bundles from established brands are fine. Bundles that appeared recently from an unfamiliar seller are a new manual process with no quality gate, and mismatched or short-counted kits are the predictable result.

Buy your back-to-school list in July rather than August. The season starts in about three weeks and the stock for it is already in the country. Stationery, backpacks, school uniform, a laptop or a tablet — the current inventory was landed at spring rates. August restocks will not be.

In an auction, decide your number before the countdown starts. With TikTok Shop’s live auctions now open to all sellers and growing several times faster than any other format, you will encounter more of them. Auctions are legitimate for genuinely scarce items — a single-run collectible, a pre-owned luxury piece, a graded card. For anything mass-produced, the scarcity is theatre and the format exists to remove your ability to comparison-shop. Write down your maximum, and treat the countdown as information about the seller rather than about the item.

Compare across EU domains before you commit. The same SKU frequently differs by twenty per cent or more between the German, French, Polish and Dutch storefronts of the same retailer, and local warehouse depth varies too. Two extra minutes on this is the highest hourly rate available in online shopping.

What is coming next

More freight increases, already announced. CMA CGM has a $4,000 per forty-foot container peak season surcharge scheduled for July 10 on transpacific lanes, and Maersk has a peak season surcharge landing a few days before that on North America services. These are on top of the July 1 increases, not instead of them. Historically other carriers match within a week or two of the first announcement. If you were tracking a specific imported item’s price, expect the next visible move in late August at the earliest — but the cost is being locked in now.

A turning point, probably, but not soon. Market analysis points to rates peaking through early July and softening in late July or August as peak-season front-loading finishes. Even the optimistic version of that leaves the second half of the year well above where autumn ranges were budgeted. And importers are already shifting priority freight into less-than-container-load shipments rather than waiting to fill a box, which tightens LCL capacity in turn.

Amazon’s New Selection Program is replaced on July 30. The refreshed version gives eligible new-brand FBA listings a different mix of launch benefits, including some fulfilment fee relief and returns support. In practice that means an unusually large wave of new-brand launches around the start of August. Some will be excellent and priced to buy market share. Some will be a first production run with no history behind it. Read the three-star reviews, and give anything genuinely new a few weeks unless the discount is large.

Southeast Asia gets a branded mall in November. TikTok Shop has confirmed a Mall format for the region with authenticity guarantees, a fifteen-day no-reason return window and free shipping, alongside a large incentive programme to recruit established sellers. For buyers in the region that is the first structural improvement in return rights on that platform, and worth waiting for on higher-value purchases.

Assistant-mediated shopping starts to be visible. With Shopify’s catalogue and protocol work shipping through the summer, more independent brands become directly queryable by AI assistants. Early on this will surface the brands with clean structured data rather than the brands with the best products — those overlap, but not perfectly. Treat an assistant’s recommendation as a shortlist, not a verdict, at least for the rest of this year.

Bottom line

Nothing about the shelf in front of you changed this week. Everything about the shelf behind it did.

The goods available today were shipped at spring freight rates by sellers who still had a safety net at the fulfilment centre. Both of those conditions ended on July 1. The replacement stock costs meaningfully more to move, takes more work to get through the door, and arrives in September into the least discount-friendly quarter of the year.

So the rule for the next few weeks is unusually simple. If it is bulky, imported, in stock, and on your list — buy it now. If it is made near you, made to order, or already second-hand — take your time, because none of this touches it. And whatever you buy, buy it from someone who already has it in a warehouse in your country, because for the rest of 2026 that is going to be the difference between a price and a surprise.
