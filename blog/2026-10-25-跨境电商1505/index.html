Cross-Border E-Commerce Pulse: October 25, 2026 - Agent Siege, TikTok 16-Nation BF, Shopify Shelf for AI

orcalva • Sun Oct 25 2026 •
#Amazon#TikTok Shop#Shopify#DTC#Black Friday 2026#AI Agents

Cross-Border E-Commerce Pulse — October 25, 2026

Quick take: As of this 23:05 refresh, October 25 is squarely inside the final listing window before the holiday storm. Two stories now define the week. First, the AI-agent access war went public: Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse shopping assistant within two weeks of launch, while Shopify, Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora and six other retailers opened their shelves to it, and card networks split on who pays when an agent errs. Second, TikTok Shop’s largest-ever Black Friday — its first covering 16 countries, including eight new European markets — has a hard full-managed listing deadline of October 27, just two days out, with a store-experience score of 3.5 now a gate to even enter. The buyer playbook this week: anchor on Amazon staples and TikTok winter-and-beauty leaders, lean on AliExpress overseas warehouses for speed, and favor DTC brands whose catalogs are machine-readable, because agent readiness is becoming the quiet filter on what is actually buyable.

1. Marketplace Platform Dynamics

Amazon North America is in full peak-season lock-down. The US Best Deal and Lightning Deal submission window closed around October 20, and the FBA inbound milestones are now behind us: October 21 for minimal shipment splits and October 28 for Amazon-optimized splits. Lightning Deals require at least 20% off the reference price and Prime Exclusive Discounts at least 15%. The 2026 deal fee structure shifted from a flat $1,000 to $100 plus 1.5% of deal sales, capped at $5,000, which rewards tighter, higher-velocity deals over blanket discounts. Holiday peak fulfillment fees are live from October 15, 2026 through January 14, 2027. Two new wrinkles this week: Amazon began testing paid review-request ads in the US (buyers who received an item seven days earlier see a request card, available only to links with fewer than 1,000 reviews), and its global consolidation warehouse in Tianjin (GXD) went live, giving North China sellers a direct FBA lane into the US and Europe.

TikTok Shop is running the broadest Black Friday in its history. The full-managed event covers 16 countries for the first time, with Poland, the Netherlands, Belgium, Czechia, Austria, Greece, Portugal and Hungary joining the European main stage alongside the existing eight. The full-managed listing hard deadline is October 27, the US cross-border POP event runs November 12 to December 1, and a store-experience score (SPS) of at least 3.5 is now a hard gate to register. Full-managed sellers were told to stage 20% of inventory by end of September, push to 80% by mid-October, and be fully stocked before Black Friday opens.

AliExpress is scaling its overseas-warehouse network up 146% year over year with official warehouses in six countries and front-loaded freight display in Europe to absorb the new EU costs, while Taobao runs its 11.11 event across 20 markets. Temu opened its warm-up around October 20 and its formal sale runs November 2 to 26, a 47-to-51-day campaign that keeps it the price floor rather than a trendsetter. Shopee and Lazada stay anchored to SEA mega-sales where free shipping plus vouchers beat brand every time.

2. Cross-Border News and Compliance

Seven developments this week change what a buyer should trust and buy.

  • The AI-agent access war is live. Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse shopping assistant within two weeks of launch, while Shopify, Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora and six other retailers partnered with it for in-chat checkout, a split that decides which catalogs AI shoppers can even see.
  • Card networks split on agent liability. American Express proposed Agent Purchase Protection and published an Intent Intelligence framework (how a shopper’s instructions are recorded before an agent buys), while JPMorgan Chase confirmed it blocks external AI agents from operating on retail customer accounts, framing the open question of who pays for an agent’s mistake.
  • Agent traffic is exploding. Cloudflare reported AI-agent requests on its network rose more than 1,700% year over year, confirming that agentic discovery is no longer a pilot but a primary front door for shopping.
  • Amazon NWD sweep. A No Wholesale Distribution purge is forcing non-brand-representative sellers to clear inventory or create removal orders before the October 27, 2026 deadline, thinning gray-market selection and improving catalog trust.
  • EU import tightening. From November 1, the EU adds an exact EUR2 Union handling fee plus EUR3 duty and a new product identification (PID) requirement, with fines up to 6% of import value; SpeedPAK began freezing a EUR2 per-parcel handling deposit on October 23 for EU-bound mail.
  • US compliance tightens at the border. The FCC votes October 29 on requiring non-MRA-lab testing for certain electronics (effective 2028), and since October 22 FDA-regulated postal freight must move through the ACE system rather than the simplified postal path, raising the risk of stalled seasonal health and beauty shipments.
  • Macro confirms a strong season. Adobe puts October US online sales at $95.8 billion, up 8%, with its two-day Prime Big Deal Days contributing about $9.9 billion, up 9.2%; the NRF expects November-December US retail of $1.013 trillion, up 4.2%; and Momentum Works forecasts TikTok Shop’s full-year 2026 GMV near $123.5 billion.

3. Hot Sectors and What Is Worth Buying

The clearest demand signal on TikTok Shop search and mall is a cluster of cold-weather and home categories: evergreen home tools, winter wear, space heaters, and Christmas trees show burst coefficients above 10 times, items priced above $60 convert better, and gift products earn about 15% more traffic. On the content side, body-related goods still drive more than two-thirds of top-tier revenue, with beauty near 30% of the total and a median price around $40. The content engine itself is compounding: US cross-border POP image-post GMV rose over 800% from May to July, short-video GMV was up more than 200% in July, and AI-assisted bulk posting crossed 100,000 items.

The buyer implication is simple. Do not chase single-occasion novelty that burns hot for seven to thirty days and collapses. Buy the evergreen tool, device, or skincare formula underneath the trend, because that is the part with a demand denominator larger than one holiday. For DTC, the products worth buying are those from brands whose catalogs are structured, schema-tagged, and agent-readable, since AI agents are now the first discovery touchpoint and the only channel some shoppers will use.

4. Independent Site (DTC) Dynamics

Direct-to-consumer is being rewired by the agent-access war faster than any platform. With Amazon walling off Muse while Shopify, Walmart, Best Buy, Gap and Sephora let it check out, the practical question for a DTC brand is no longer “should I be on AI channels” but “can agents read me and can they pay for me.” Shopify’s Catalog now feeds compare-at prices to agents (Google AI Mode, Microsoft Copilot, Meta Muse) as of October 9, and Shop Pre-Verification (October 5) syncs carts across devices with one-tap Shop Pay. Cloudflare’s 1,700% agent-traffic jump means the catalog that is clean wins the shelf.

The operational response is threefold: set accurate compare-at prices so agents can see your markdown, complete the three required policies (terms, privacy, returns) that every AI channel demands, and file trademarks before competitors capture the agent-surfaced shelf space. A warning signal from the UK market: Class 35 trademark filings fell 95.2% in Q4 2026 even as AI rewires discovery, and 28% of shoppers now deliberately leave carts to wait for a price drop. The brands that win are the ones agents can read, price, and trust.

5. Breakout Picks: Amazon, Shopify, TikTok Shop

  • Amazon breakouts. Korean beauty (medicube, d’Alba), portable power and charging, and multi-function kitchen appliances lead because they combine high margin, giftability, and year-round demand. The new paid review-request ad test favors newer sub-1,000-review links, so fresh brands in these categories get a discovery tailwind.
  • Shopify and DTC breakouts. Brands with agent-ready catalogs (Ridge, Tabs, Gymshark) and sustainability positioning (Allbirds) are best positioned, because agents surface them directly into checkout and compare-at prices now advertise the discount.
  • TikTok Shop breakouts. Winter wear, heated comfort goods, beauty advent calendars, and LED ambient lighting are the content-machine favorites across the new 16-country footprint, amplified by Fulfilled-by-TikTok speed badges and the platform’s billion-level holiday exposure push, with SPS 3.5 as the entry toll.

8. Forward Outlook

The next thirty days decide the quarter. TikTok Shop’s BFCM runs November 11 to 30 for the full-managed event and November 12 to December 1 for the US POP window, Amazon Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday November 30, and more than 60% of US shoppers are expected to have carts built before the end of October, so front-loading is already underway. The hard gates stack up fast: the TikTok full-managed listing wall on October 27, the Amazon NWD removal deadline the same day, the EU product-identification and handling-fee rule on November 1, and Shopify’s recommended agentic settings freeze by November 19. Agent traffic up 1,700% means the brands with clean, discounted, policy-complete catalogs capture discovery while the rest stay invisible.

The worth-buying trajectory is consistent: evergreen home tools, winter-comfort goods, beauty staples, and DTC brands optimized for machine readability will hold value past the holiday spike, while single-occasion novelties will fade the moment the calendar turns and the agent shelf re-sorts.

9. Bottom Line

Buy the durable product underneath the trend, not the dated novelty on top of it. Anchor carts on Amazon staples and TikTok winter-and-beauty leaders this week, lean on AliExpress overseas warehouses for speed, use Temu only as the price-check terminal, and favor DTC brands whose catalogs AI agents can read, price, and check out. Watch the October 27 TikTok listing wall and Amazon NWD deadline and the November 1 EU import rule, because compliance and agent-readiness, not price, will be the quiet filter on what is actually available to buy this season.
