Best Things to Buy Right Now — Week of July 5, 2026 Shopping Guide

orcalva • Wed Nov 09 2022 •
#shopping guide#best buys#eu parcel duty#cross-border shipping#marketplace picks#dtc brands#amazon latam#china tax compliance#fast fashion#de minimis

Quick take: The era of the €2 gadget arriving free from across the world just ended in Europe. As of July 1, 2026, the EU charges €3 on every parcel worth €150 or less coming from outside the bloc — and it is levied per product category inside the parcel, not per parcel. Cheap trinkets just got 50–100% more expensive overnight, while a €100 item barely notices. The smart move this week is to buy fewer, better, branded, and local: first-party European retailers, brands that already hold EU stock, and — for a bright spot — the suddenly fast Chinese-brand pipeline into Brazil and Mexico.

What actually changed

Three things landed in the same first week of July, and they all point the same direction: the anonymous, no-name, ship-from-China parcel is being priced out.

1. The EU’s €3 parcel duty went live on July 1. This is the big one. Any parcel valued at €150 or less entering the EU from outside now carries a flat €3 charge. The detail that matters most for shoppers: the fee is applied per distinct product category inside the parcel, based on customs classification — so an order of “one phone case, one sock, one keychain” can trip three separate charges, not one. Worked example: a €9 order split across three categories pays €9 in duty — doubling the price — while a single €9 item in one category pays €3, and a €90 item in one category pays €3 (about 3%). In other words, the duty is brutally regressive: it punishes the cheap and spares the considered. For context, around 4.6 billion parcels under €150 reached the EU in 2024, and the overwhelming majority originated in China. The duty is a provisional measure while a permanent customs system is built, but it is real money today.

2. Platforms are now “deemed importers.” Alongside the duty, the rule shift makes the marketplace itself responsible for the duty and for product safety. That means toys, electronics, and baby goods now need proper CE / GPSR compliance documentation to be listed at all. The flood of uncertified no-name items is receding, and the warranties on what remains are suddenly worth reading.

3. The supply side is tightening at the same time. In the same window, one major marketplace pushed its first-quarter China-seller transaction data to Chinese tax authorities and began broader historical audits, with tax offices contacting sellers directly. Small operators who lived on thin margins and zero declarations are being squeezed from both ends — duty on the way in, tax on the way out. The practical result for shoppers: expect fewer fly-by-night listings and more pressure on price.

Two more signals frame the moment. A large fast-fashion platform was fined under the EU’s Digital Services Act earlier this year (May), and France has advanced an anti-fast-fashion law that adds an eco-levy and restricts advertising for the cheapest ultra-fast fashion. Regulators are signalling that further platform-level enforcement is coming through the month.

The organising idea for this week: “consolidate or pay twice.” Because the €3 is per category, bundling ten different knick-knacks into one cart can cost €30 in fees. A single durable item priced above ~€40 absorbs the duty into rounding error. So the buying rule flips: stop scattering tiny orders, start concentrating on fewer, better things.

Amazon, Shopify and TikTok

Amazon feels the duty most on its third-party, China-direct listings — the ones that previously shipped a $3 cable straight to your door. Stock that sits in Amazon’s own EU fulfilment centres is unaffected, which is why “ships from Amazon EU” is suddenly the line to look for. The same week, Amazon pushed harder into Latin America (more on that below) and continued its China-seller tax-data submissions, so the platform is pulling in two directions at once: cleaner and more local in Europe, more aggressive in emerging markets.

Shopify sellers are the quiet winners. A direct-to-consumer brand that already holds inventory in a US or EU warehouse ships to you with no €3 parcel surcharge and a real return address. This week’s DTC list is built around exactly that: brands whose dispatch promise is theirs to keep. If a Shopify brand ships from China to the EU, it eats the same duty — so the “ships from” line is the whole game.

TikTok Shop is where the contradiction is sharpest. In the US the chart keeps fragmenting, with virality spreading across more small sellers and shorter half-lives per product. In Europe, livestream selling of ultra-cheap fashion is now squarely in the regulators’ sights — France’s ad restrictions hit exactly the “influencer pushes a €4 dress” playbook. The takeaway: a TikTok deal is still a deal, but verify the ship-from country and the return path before you tap buy.

How to buy well this week

  1. Consolidate categories, not just parcels. The €3 is charged per product category in the parcel. Ten different tiny items = up to ten charges. One order, one or two categories, one fee.
  2. Use the €40 rule. Below about €5, the duty is a dealbreaker (100%+). Above about €40, it is rounding error. Spend the money on fewer, better things.
  3. Prefer first-party or local-warehouse sellers. Bol, Coolblue, Otto, Boulanger and the DTC brands above hold stock in-region — no parcel duty, real warranty, easy returns.
  4. Read the “ships from” line. On any marketplace, “ships from China” to an EU address now carries the €3 (often per category). “Ships from [EU country]” does not.
  5. Check CE / GPSR marks on toys, electronics, and baby goods. Compliance is now enforced at listing, so the mark is a real signal, not decoration.
  6. Latin America shortcut: if you are shopping in Brazil or Mexico, look for listings backed by upgraded north-to-south fulfilment — Chinese-brand gear (chargers, cameras, collectibles) is now landing in 1–7 days (Mexico) and about 10 days (Brazil) from regional stock.

What is coming next

  • More platform-level enforcement. Regulators have signalled further record fines under the Digital Services Act are likely through the month, after an earlier fast-fashion penalty. Expect tighter listing controls, not looser.
  • France’s anti-fast-fashion law rolls out. The eco-levy plus ad restrictions target the cheapest ultra-fast fashion — pushing demand toward branded outlets (see Zalando Lounge above).
  • Permanent EU customs reform. The provisional €3 is a placeholder; the permanent system (a central customs data hub and platform-held import responsibility) phases in over the next couple of years. The duty is not going away.
  • Chinese platforms localise or lose. Expect the big China-based marketplaces to accelerate EU warehouse build-out so more SKUs ship from inside the bloc and dodge the parcel charge. The “ship-from-China” share of your feed will shrink.
  • Latin America stays the growth frontier. The 3,000-brand push into Brazil and Mexico runs through the second half of 2026, so LATAM shoppers get progressively faster, cheaper access to global brands.

The bottom line

Shop like it’s 2019: fewer things, better things, branded things, local things. The €3 parcel duty did not kill cross-border shopping — it killed the frivolous version of it. The winners this week are first-party European retailers, DTC brands with regional warehouses, and the unexpectedly fast Latin American pipeline. Everything else just got €3 heavier per category, and that math does not lie.
