Bundles are frozen absolutely through the final day

Independent Shopify DTC stores rotated standalone acquisition offers into bundles at the month turn, settled them through March 2, froze them through the prior weekend and confirmed the minor tweaks across last week. On March 14 that settled state is at the close of the weekend freeze, and because these stores reprice manually rather than algorithmically, the freeze is absolute - no engine adjusts a sticker overnight, and no human does either on a Sunday. Effective first-order cost sits stable above the February 28 low but well below the confirmed marketplace equivalents, and it will hold through to Monday’s open. The clearest DTC price of the freeze week is now behind us, but the gap to the frozen Sunday number is trivial and the floor is intact and held through the weekend.

Extended return windows persist at the five-step gradient, no new store turns on

The DTC signal that began on the largest stores, broadened across the mid-size cohort and widened through last week to a five-step gradient is the genuine independent-side movement, but the weekend freeze means no new store turns on a window until Monday - the broadening is a weekday behaviour. Because the signal is per-store policy rather than a cohort-wide mechanic, the work stays local: checking the actual policy page per store. The direction is unambiguous and it releases the gift-intent demand the frozen weekend held back, one tier at a time. The practical split holds: gift-intent purchases at stores that have already extended can release, while the rest of the cohort stays a self-use decision until their windows follow through. This is a broadening release, not a general one, and the freeze merely holds the gradient in place.

Regional stock holds the edge as the lanes complete normalization

The DTC delivery advantage was at its most concrete during the peak and narrows slightly as the surge clears, but it does not disappear. A regionally stocked DTC order still means two-to-five-day delivery, no cross-border latency and a flat shipped price, against a marketplace lane that is now normalized rather than fast. For known-specification self-use items, the DTC sweet spot holds: launch-priced goods at near-staple speed on a frozen price, with the option to decide over the weekend without penalty. The congestion was the urgent reason to prefer DTC last week; the structural reasons - flat pricing, no repricing engine, regional speed, and the broadening return windows - are why the preference persists and holds frozen through the weekend.
