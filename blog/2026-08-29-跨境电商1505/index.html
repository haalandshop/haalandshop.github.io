Executive Summary

The cross-border board on 2026-08-29 is the first Saturday after the 828 mega-sale closed, and that single fact reframes the whole day. Friday was the sale, Saturday is the comedown, and the comedown is where the edge lives. When a mega-sale shuts, sellers restore base prices fast - usually inside 24 hours - but the restoration is uneven. Delivered totals, shipping tiers, cross-listed variants and un-enrolled colourways lag by two to three days. August 29 sits at T-plus-one of that lag, which means the cheapest, lowest-risk edge on this report costs nothing but attention: compare the restored base price to the still-lagging delivered total on the same SKU.

Three currents define the date, and the order matters.

First, the T-plus-three repricing lag window is open. This is the verification pass, not the setup. The 828 sale closed August 28, so August 29 through 31 is the live window where base prices have snapped back but delivered totals have not. Buyers who compare base price to delivered total on the same SKU can still catch the mismatch.

Second, the five-day countdown to the Sep 3 drone and RC tariff step begins today. On September 3, drone and remote-control tariff lines step to 100 percent and 25 percent respectively as a permanent measure. That is five trading days of front-run opportunity on affected categories, and five days of last-chance pricing before the step lands.

Third, the 9.9 voucher-cap versus 11.11 hold decision is now a live buyer choice. Shopee and Lazada open their 9.9 peak on September 8, with the Southeast Asia payday voucher window already active. Buyers must decide whether a fungible volume item is cheaper at 9.9 or worth waiting for 11.11, and the answer differs by category and by whether the item is fungible or specific.

Behind all three sits the standing backdrop only: the de-minimis anniversary lands today as one full year, but per the corrected timeline it is context, not a price mechanic. The corrected tariff sequence remains the reference frame - EO 14256 for CN and Hong Kong on 2025-05-02, EO 14324 for all countries on 2025-08-29, the CBP regulation on 2026-06-24, postal informal entry on 2026-07-24, the OBBBA statutory repeal effective 2027-07-01, EU 150 EUR relief abolished on 2026-07-01 with a flat 3 EUR per tariff sub-heading rather than per parcel, the EU Union handling fee earliest in November 2026, the five-tier structure from 2028-07-01, and the UK 135 GBP threshold still in consultation around 2029.

Heating up on August 29: kitchen and coffee adjacent formats, outdoor and camping lighting, garden and grow lighting, beauty and glow serums, pet cooling, and cordless home appliances ahead of Labor Day. These sectors share one trait - they are fungible enough to reprice fast but variant-rich enough that delivered totals lag, which is exactly where the T-plus window pays.

Forward Outlook: Worth-Buying Trajectory Into September

The trajectory from August 29 into September is defined by three dated walls and one decision. The first wall is Sep 1, the Amazon EU 7-day inbound window plus MRN or EORI on Pan-EU and France. Fungible EU-bound volume should be bought or already warehoused before Tuesday; anything relying on post-Tuesday inbound faces friction priced into delivery.

The second wall is Sep 3, the drone and RC tariff step to 100 percent and 25 percent as a permanent measure. Affected categories reprice structurally on September 3; the five-day countdown from August 29 is the last clean buy window for RC-adjacent goods at pre-step pricing. After the step, those lines are a hold, not a buy.

The third wall is the 9.9 peak on September 8 to 9, with Shopee and Lazada peaking together and AliExpress Fall Sale spanning September 15 to 21, Brand Day September 23 to 29, and US Labor Day on September 7. The appliance strike around Labor Day favours cordless home formats bought before the holiday.

The Five-Day Tariff Countdown

From August 29 the clock to the Sep 3 drone and RC step is exactly five trading days. The discipline is simple: buy RC-adjacent and drone-adjacent goods you already decided on before September 3, at last-chance pricing through September 2, and do not initiate new RC-category buys after the step. The Elmsford lantern is the proxy, not the only item; the step reprices the whole line.

The 9.9 Versus 11.11 Hold Decision

The hold decision is category-specific. Fungible volume items - pet mats, cutlery sets, cables, linens - almost always net cheaper at 9.9 once payday and event vouchers stack, and waiting for 11.11 rarely beats the combined 9.9 discount plus shipping. Specific variants, single-run goods and high-ticket items still favour 11.11 depth. The rule: apply the hold decision only to purchases you have already decided on, and let the fungible-versus-specific split - badge it on the SKU, not the page - drive the call.

Buyer Risk and Compliance Notes

Three risks sit under every buy on August 29. The first is the Sep 1 Amazon EU inbound friction: assume tighter Pan-EU and France handling after Tuesday and do not commit fungible EU volume to post-window inbound without regional stock. The second is the Sep 3 tariff step: RC and drone lines reprice permanently, so any affected new buy after the step carries the step cost. The third is landed-cost drift from the postal informal entry date of 2026-07-24, the CBP Entry Type 13 voluntary mail test on September 22, and postal full compliance on October 22; single-origin CN post is the most exposed.

Compliance discipline for the buyer: verify CE or FCC where the category requires it, confirm the EU 150 EUR relief is already abolished so per-sub-heading 3 EUR handling applies to EU-bound goods, and treat the UK 135 GBP threshold as still in consultation rather than a live relief. None of these block the 20 picks above, but they frame the landed cost that determines whether a pick stays worth buying after delivery.
