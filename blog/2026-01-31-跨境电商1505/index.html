Executive Summary

The cross-border board on 2026-01-31 is the last Saturday before two dated walls and one holiday window converge, and that single fact defines the whole day. The Shopee and Lazada 2.2 mega-sale fires February 2, so January 31 is T-minus-two of the pre-add window - the stretch where shoppers load carts and let platform plus voucher math stack before the double-date pricing goes live. Sitting behind that is the Chinese New Year factory shutdown, which in 2026 lands around February 17 and silences CN-origin production for roughly two weeks; with the wind-down starting in the first week of February, January 31 is effectively the final low-traffic weekend to buy CN-origin fungibles that are already warehoused in-region rather than still at the factory gate. By next weekend the air-freight lane has closed and fresh supply is a March story.

Three currents move the date, and the order matters.

First, the pre-Chinese-New-Year stock wall is hardening. This is the last-chance pass, not the setup. CN-origin factories are already throttling new orders with February delivery promises, and the ocean bookings that would arrive post-holiday have not been placed. January 31 favours goods that cleared customs and sit in FBA or a regional 3PL today; anything still at the factory gate becomes a hold after this week.

Second, the 2.2 SEA mega-sale pre-add is live and accelerating. Shopee and Lazada open the double-date event February 2, with voucher pools and payday credits already stacking on pre-added carts. January 31 is the cleanest day to pre-add fungible electronics and home-entertainment goods before the sale fires and the best voucher depth opens - the buy signal is the pre-add, not the wait.

Third, the Valentine’s and Super Bowl LX cut-offs are both inside two weeks. Valentine’s Day is February 14, so cross-border gifting must clear customs and last-mile before roughly February 10, and small air-friendly parcels win while heavy goods lose. Super Bowl LX is February 8, so game-day hosting and party decor peak this week and then drop sharply after the game. January 31 is the Saturday that serves both: a gifting parcel and a game-day cart can be built in the same session.

Sector Rotation: What Is Heating Up and Cooling Down

Heating up on January 31: Valentine’s gifting and self-care, small air-friendly beauty and jewelry, CN-origin fungibles already warehoused in-region, 2.2 pre-add electronics and home-entertainment, Super Bowl game-day small appliances and party decor, and winter home comfort that survives the season transition. These sectors share one trait - they are small enough to air-freight before the lane closes and specific enough to carry a dated-event hook that expires inside two weeks.

Cooling down: heavy furniture and oversized goods are cooling because the CNY blackout and the air-premium math make any February arrival expensive and uncertain; single-variant RC and outdoor toys are cooling as winter clears and the post-holiday restock resets the category; and pure trend apparel without a season-transition or event hook is weakening as winter inventory clears.

Cooling Sectors to Avoid

Skip oversized and heavy goods with a CN-origin February delivery promise unless already warehoused in-region - the blackout plus air premium reprices the landed cost structurally. Skip single-variant RC toys that have no event hook; the category resets after the holidays and the restock lands in March. Skip generic trend apparel with no season-transition or Valentine’s logic; the Knit Sweater Dress works because it bridges winter to spring, but a plain summer tee does not.

Goods already in FBA or a regional 3PL are insulated; goods that are not become a hold after this week.

Buyer Risk and Compliance Notes

Three risks sit under every buy on January 31.
